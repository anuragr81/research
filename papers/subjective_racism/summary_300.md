# Social Discrimination as Informationally Mediated Closure

**300-Word Summary**

Anurag Srivastava · Riskcare Ltd., London · University of Reading

---

When people encounter someone they have not met before, they read soft signals — accent, dress, name, consumption patterns — and use them to form beliefs about that person's economic position. The inference rule governing this process has a critical property: individual counter-examples do not revise group beliefs. A high-class member of a historically disadvantaged group does not change what observers believe about the group. He is treated as an exception. The stereotype persists. This is not irrationality. It is the mathematically natural way to update beliefs from ambiguous evidence, and it generates stereotype persistence as a theorem.

This paper builds a formal economic model on this foundation. The starting point — that economic class generates social clustering — is a premise. What the model proves is stronger: the inference process generates a self-sustaining trap as a stable equilibrium outcome, even in a society with no racial divisions at all. Racial discrimination enters as a perturbation of this already-operating equilibrium. The model takes no position on whether class or racial exploitation came first historically; the mechanism operates identically under either account.

The model shows that individual compliance with host-group markers — however extensive — produces personal reclassification without group-level liberation. The colonial évolué, the converso, and the anglicised colonial subject all occupied a structural zone of partial inclusion that fell short of integration by construction of the inference rule, not by failure of individual effort. The compliance route is individually rational but collectively self-defeating: it channels effort into personal reclassification while leaving the group prior unchanged.

When race and gender markers compound in institutional settings — harassment accusations, credibility assessments, disciplinary proceedings — the resulting distortion exceeds what either dimension produces alone. Anti-discrimination policy targeting one dimension leaves the interaction term untouched.

The welfare loss includes a component that no formal policy instrument can eliminate while social interaction relies on soft signals. This characterises the limits of instruments, not a verdict on the world. The only durable formal remedy operates on intergenerational economic mobility itself.
