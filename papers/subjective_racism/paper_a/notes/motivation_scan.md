# Motivation scan, 15 October 2026

The author asked what research problem Paper A1 solves, and how class matters
in social interaction in the existing literature. Three searches were run on
the web, and two papers were then read in full by hand. On 16 October 2026
fifteen sources nearest the gap were read in full and given records under
`lit/`. A source with a record is marked [F] below and cited by its record. Any
other source still needs a full read and a record before a manuscript row rests
on it.

Access marks: **[F]** full text read, **[P]** the quoted passage read in the
source, **[A]** abstract or summary only, **[S]** quoted through a secondary
source only.

## What the literature already has

| Point | Sources |
|---|---|
| Class is read from markers such as dialect, dress and manners, and the markers can be used to misrepresent class. | Goffman (1951) [S, Spanish full text]; Labov (1966) [F]; Kraus et al. (2019) [F]; Kraus, Park and Tan (2017) [F]; Accent Bias Britain (2020) [F] |
| Observers impute a group's characteristics to its members, and a noisier group is penalised. | Phelps (1972), Aigner and Cain (1977) through Fang and Moro (2011) [F] |
| Signals sort partners in social matching, and payoff-irrelevant attributes can gain value there. | Pesendorfer (1995) [F]; Cole, Mailath and Postlewaite (1991 WP) [F]; Mailath and Postlewaite (2006 WP) [F]; Rege (2008) [A]; Eeckhout (2006) [F]; Pęski and Szentes (2012 WP) [F] |
| Passing selects the abler members into the group with the greater return to skill. | Kim and Loury (2012, KDI J. Econ. Policy) [F]; Eguia (2013 WP) [F]; evidence in Dahis, Nix and Qian (2019) [F], Mill and Stein (2012) [F], Grogger (2011) [A] |

Kim and Loury (2012), verbatim: "when group membership is endogenous then the
logic of individuals' identity choices leads there to be a positive selection of
higher ability individuals into the group with a better reputation" (KLI-Q1).
Their Proposition 2 sends the able into the group with the greater return to
skill, R(π), rather than the higher belief π (KLI-Q11, KLI-D2). R falls above
its peak, and their own Figure 3 draws a case with the better-regarded group
offering the lower return. Their setting is wages. Row M14 is a special case of
theirs in a contact setting only where the two groups are ranked the same way by
belief and by return.

Mailath and Postlewaite (2006 WP): agents see income and match on it. An
attribute such as accent gains value through the matching equilibrium, not by
signalling unseen income. The authors write that "there is no asymmetric
information, hence no role for signalling" (MP-Q14). The earlier versions of
2002 and 2004 agree on this point (MP-D9, MP-D10). The published version is not
read.

## The empirical hook

Chetty et al. (2022, Social Capital II) [F]: "The other half is explained by
friending bias—the tendency for low-SES people to befriend high-SES people at
lower rates even conditional on exposure." And: "Friending bias is also higher
in more racially diverse schools." Their mechanism for friending bias is open.
Their only cost evidence is a suggestion about fraternity dues.

McPherson et al. (2001) [F]: "areas where African Americans are a larger part of
the population also show larger African-American/Anglo differences in
education, income and other social class variables."

Evidence on whether the racial penalty in contact is inferred class is split.
For it are Crabtree et al. (2022) [F] on names read as class, and Laouénan and
Rathelot (2022) [A] on Airbnb. Against it are Hitsch, Hortaçsu and Ariely
(2006 WP) [F] on dating, May and Goldsmith (2018) [A] on nightclub doors, and
Bertrand and Mullainathan (2004) [F] on names in hiring.

## Candidate contributions after the full reads of 16 October 2026

Fifteen records, listed in `lit/README.md`. Each verdict below cites the
quotation that carries it.

1. **Group avoidance derived from class homophily.** In expected class distance
   the cost of a group is the squared gap between its mean and the observer's
   own class, plus its within-group variance. No source read has this
   dependence on the observer's own class, and no source read has a group's
   spread penalised in matching.
   - Every source read with preferences over partners makes them vertical, so
     that all observers rank partners alike. Pesendorfer (PF-Q6), Cole, Mailath
     and Postlewaite (CM-Q2), Mailath and Postlewaite (MP-Q5), Eguia (EG-Q6) and
     Craig and Fryer (CF-Q10) all assume this.
   - Pęski and Szentes state the claim that contribution 1 qualifies. A bias of
     each colour against the other "is inconsistent with statistical
     discrimination because the signal value of colour must be the same for any
     employer, regardless of his own colour" (PS-Q4, again as PS-Q14). Under
     class homophily the posterior is common to all observers, but the decision
     it supports depends on the observer's own class. Whether the model yields
     avoidance of each group by the other is inferred here and not yet checked
     in Lean.
   - The variance appears in three places, none as a penalty in matching.
     Charles, Hurst and Roussanov name it and leave it out (CH-Q4). Moav and
     Neeman note that under pooling the group's distribution would enter the
     belief (MN-Q9). Fryer and Jackson make a pooled category less accurately
     predicted (FJ-Q1, FJ-Q2), but the observer's own type does not enter.
2. **Budget compatibility as the reason class matters.** No source read has
   it. Assortative matching comes from vertical preference and stability in
   Mailath and Postlewaite (MP-Q5) and in Cole, Mailath and Postlewaite
   (CM-Q6). That account is the rival A1 must set its own against. Pesendorfer
   doubts complementarity when the type is wealth that couples pool (PF-Q14),
   and A1 must say why budget compatibility is not that pooling.
3. **Venue exclusion as the aggregate of client inference, and price
   screening as class certification.** Price screening in matching is not
   new. Pesendorfer has a price that separates high types from low (PF-Q2,
   PF-Q3) and a seller valued by the average type of its clients (PF-Q15).
   Corneo and Jeanne have a good whose purchase certifies membership in the
   richest n (CJ-Q5). Austen-Smith and Fryer have a group that admits by a
   threshold on beliefs (AS-Q6), and Eguia has an entry cost that screens on
   unseen skill (EG-Q1, EG-Q9). Venue exclusion built up from clients'
   inferences from a group marker is in none of them. Under A1's utility a low
   class does not want a high pool, so the reason Pesendorfer needs a price is
   absent and A1 must give its own.
4. **A test of the class channel.** No source read states it for contact. Two
   sources state the rival prediction, a penalty that survives when the trait
   is shown. These are Fryer and Jackson (FJ-Q11) and Mailath and Postlewaite
   (MP-Q1 to MP-Q3). The coordination channels of Pęski and Szentes and of
   Eeckhout predict the same. In labour markets the vanishing of the group
   penalty once type is shown is an assumption of Kim and Loury (KLB-Q5) and a
   result of their market learning (KLA-Q6, KLA-Q14). Charles, Hurst and
   Roussanov find that the race gap in visible spending vanishes given the
   own-group mean income (CH-Q10), which is a test on the sender's spending
   rather than on the observer's contact.

An equilibrium condition that a group's imputed standing equal the actual mean
of its members is in Kim and Loury (KLI-Q10), Austen-Smith and Fryer (AS-Q5),
Corneo and Jeanne (eq. 8), Eguia (eq. 3) and Craig and Fryer (CF-Q4, CF-Q5).
A1 states this condition for an exogenous class, rather than for a chosen skill
or a chosen good, and cites them for it.

## Not new

- Row M14, passing lowers the visible mean, is in Kim and Loury (2012) with the
  qualification above, and selective exit lowering a group's average skill is
  in Eguia (`sD_lt_half`).
- "Soft evidence" and "soft marker" have no formal content once Jeffrey's
  rule is dropped. What remains is a noisy group marker under group
  sufficiency.

## Premises of A1 that sources read do not share

- Eguia gives both backgrounds the same distribution of ability (EG-Q3), so
  his argument against barriers on immutable traits (EG-Q14) does not carry
  over to A1.
- In Charles, Hurst and Roussanov a richer own group lowers a member's status
  (CH-Q6). In A1 a richer group raises the class imputed to a member. Moav and
  Neeman describe that paper as showing race to be a signal of income (MN-Q3),
  which the working paper read does not support (MN-D5). A1 should not repeat
  the description.
- Eeckhout writes that "in the standard model of statistical discrimination,
  size does not matter" (EE-Q3). A1 has to say whether group size enters its
  equilibrium.
