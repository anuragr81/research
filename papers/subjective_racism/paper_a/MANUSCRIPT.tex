\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Question}

% The question is the author's choice of 15 October 2026 (TODO.md, D2).
When does reading class from group markers trap a group at a low standing, and
how much mobility does it take to get out? An observer who cannot see a
person's class reads it from the markers of a group, and the group's standing,
the mean class of its members, in turn moves with how often its members are
refused. The question asks when this loop holds a group at a low standing
rather than letting it rise to integration, and what mobility breaks the loop.

\subsection{Answer}

A group is trapped when two conditions hold together. The observer's belief
about the group follows the group's standing from one generation to the next,
and mobility is below the mobility threshold
$\tilde\gamma^\ast=b-2\sqrt{\mu b}$, where $b$ is the exclusion pressure,
salience times the cost of exclusion, and $\mu$ is the convergence rate (M9).
The group then has a closure trap and a tipping point, and under the bounds of
M6 every group that starts below the tipping point ends in the trap (M5, M6).
Without the first condition there is no trap, since a belief frozen at its
first value gives one fixed point and no tipping point (M7, M8). Without the
second, integration is the only fixed point (M9). The threshold is positive
only when $b>4\mu$, so a group under weaker exclusion pressure is never
trapped, and more salient markers raise the threshold wherever it is positive
(M9, M10). These statements are for the canonical discrimination-rate
function, and for a general one a standing at which the composed map dips
below the diagonal gives a trap (M12).

Mobility above the threshold gets every group out. Integration is then the
only fixed point, and under the bounds of M6 every standing converges to it
(M9, M11). Just above the threshold the way out is slow, since crossing the
window about the merged root takes at least $1/(\sqrt b\sqrt\varepsilon)$
generations when mobility exceeds the threshold by $\varepsilon$ (M16). A
one-off rise in standing is no substitute for mobility. Below the threshold
it gets a group out only if it lifts the group above the tipping point, and a
rise that leaves the group below the tipping point returns it to the trap
(M6). Nor is the exit of individual members the group's escape. When the
better-off members pass, the visible group's mean class falls (M14).

\label{app:proofs}

\paragraph{Notation.} A group is $g$. Economic class takes values in a finite
set $\mathcal{C}$. The prior $\pi_g(c)=P(c\mid g)$ is the observer's
distribution of class within group $g$. The marker is $m$, and its likelihood
is $\ell_g(c)=P(m\mid c,g)$. The marginal of the marker within the group is
$Z_g=\sum_{c'\in\mathcal{C}}\ell_g(c')\,\pi_g(c')=P(m\mid g)$, and the
Bayesian posterior on class is $\pi'_g(c)=\ell_g(c)\,\pi_g(c)/Z_g=P(c\mid m,g)$.
Rows M2 and M3 range over a finite set of groups $\mathcal{G}$. The
observer's weights on the groups are $q(g)$ before the marker is read, the
marginal of the marker is
$Z=\sum_{g}\sum_{c}\ell_g(c)\,\pi_g(c)\,q(g)=P(m)$, the Bayesian posterior on
group is $q'(g)=\sum_{c}\ell_g(c)\,\pi_g(c)\,q(g)/Z$, and the Bayesian
posterior on class is $P(c\mid m)=\sum_{g}\ell_g(c)\,\pi_g(c)\,q(g)/Z$. In M3,
$w$ is a target revision of the group weights and $\kappa$ a scale.

Rows M4 to M6 concern a group's standing $\bar c\in[0,1]$, the mean class of
the group, which the observer's prior re-learns each generation
(Appendix~\ref{app:scope}). The parameters are the convergence rate $\mu$,
the mobility $\tilde\gamma$, the salience $s_g$, the cost of exclusion $\delta$
and the discrimination-rate function $F$, and $b=s_g\delta$ is the exclusion
pressure. The composed map is
$\mathcal{H}_F(\bar c)=\mu+\bar c\,(1-\mu+\tilde\gamma-s_g\delta F(\bar c))
-\tilde\gamma\,\bar c^2$. With $F(\bar c)=(1-\bar c)^2$ and $b=s_g\delta$ it is
$\mathcal{H}(\bar c)=\mu+\bar c\,(1-\mu+\tilde\gamma-b(1-\bar c)^2)
-\tilde\gamma\,\bar c^2$, and $Q(\bar c)=b\bar c^2-(b-\tilde\gamma)\bar c+\mu$.
$\mathcal{H}^n$ is the $n$-th iterate of $\mathcal{H}$.

\sum_{(j,i)\in S\times P}(c_i-c_j)=|S|\sum_{i\in P}c_i-|P|\sum_{j\in S}c_j ,
\]
which is $\ge0$ under the ordering and $>0$ if one term is. With
$\sum_V=\sum_S+\sum_P$ and $|V|=|S|+|P|$,
\[
\bar c(S)\le\bar c(V)
\iff|V|\sum_Sc\le|S|\sum_Vc
\iff|P|\sum_Sc\le|S|\sum_Pc ,
\]
and the same with $<$.
(ii) $\mathrm{cost}(y)\le\mathrm{cost}(x)\le\mathrm{gain}(x)\le\mathrm{gain}(y)$.
(iii) $\bar c'\le\bar c\Rightarrow F(\bar c)\le F(\bar c')$, and $s_g\ge0$.
Control. $V=\{0,1\}$, $c=(0,1)$, $P=\{0\}$. $\bar c(V\setminus P)=1$ and
$\bar c(V)=\tfrac12$.
\end{mproof}

$\tilde\gamma=(u-v)^2-\mu+\varepsilon$, $b-\tilde\gamma=2uv-\varepsilon$ and
\[

\]
Window. $e\le v$ gives $c_0-h\ge0$, and $v+e\le u$ gives $c_0+h\le1$. For
$\bar c$ in the window, $|u\bar c-v|\le e$, so
\[
0\le Q(\bar c)\le e^2+\varepsilon\bar c\le2\varepsilon,
\qquad
0\le\mathcal{H}(\bar c)-\bar c=(1-\bar c)Q(\bar c)\le2\varepsilon .
\]

\[

\iff\frac{e}{u}\le e^2n
\iff\frac1{ue}\le n .
\]

$\tfrac12\le\sqrt{3/10}\le\tfrac59$, so $\tfrac14+\tfrac1{10}\le\sqrt{3/10}$
and $1/(\sqrt{3/10}\cdot\tfrac1{10})\ge18$.
Control. $\mathcal{H}^0$ is the identity, and $ue>0$.
\end{mproof}

\section{Primitives and scope}
\label{app:scope}

\paragraph{Class space.} Class takes finitely many values in the Lean
statement of every row so far. Row M1 depends on this. The continuous class of
the predecessor manuscript lies outside the rows until a row states it.

\paragraph{Group sufficiency.} The predecessor manuscript states the
assumption as conditional independence, $c\perp m\mid g$, ``equivalently''
the posterior form $P(c\mid m,g)=P(c\mid g)$, and uses the likelihood form
$P(m\mid c,g)=P(m\mid g)$ in the proof of its structural-trap proposition.
Row M1 proves the likelihood and posterior forms equivalent when the prior
gives every class positive probability, and its second control shows the
posterior form is weaker without that support.

\paragraph{The updating rule.} The observer updates by Bayes' rule alone, a
choice the author made on 14 October 2026 because of rows M2 and M3. The
predecessor manuscript adds Jeffrey's rule of conditioning in the form of its
equation (kinematics), which mixes the within-group class distributions by
group weights taken as given, as Jeffrey's own rule mixes conditionals on
the atoms of a partition (L6). Under group sufficiency, the Bayesian
posterior on class is such a mixture (M2), and every such mixture whose prior
gives each group positive weight is a Bayesian posterior for some marker
likelihood (M3). Within one reading of a marker the two rules therefore
produce the same posteriors on class, and a second rule adds no result.
Across several readings the two can differ, since two applications of
Jeffrey's rule need not commute (L7), and the model has one reading.

\paragraph{The law of motion.} Rows M4 to M17 take the predecessor's
composed map, its equation (composed), as the law of motion of a group's
standing, rather than deriving it. The engagement decision that would derive
it, with the predecessor's risk-neutral payoff in squared class distance, its
Beta law for class within a group and its engagement threshold, lies upstream
of $F$, and $F$ is taken as given. The rate of a generation is computed from
that generation's standing, which is what the predecessor's timescale
separation provides.

\paragraph{Corrections to the predecessor's propositions.} Four of the
predecessor's propositions hold only with hypotheses it does not state. Its
``sufficiently large $\delta$'' for the trap is bounded above as well as
below, since the basins of M6 need $s_g\delta<1-\mu+\tilde\gamma$, the first
control of M6 shows the map leaving $[0,1]$ when $s_g\delta$ is large, and
the third shows the basin result failing above the bound. Its positive
mobility threshold needs $s_g\delta>4\mu$ (M9), the value of $b^\ast$ at zero
mobility (M10). Its salience corollary holds for $s_g\delta\ge\mu$ rather than
for every salience, and so wherever the threshold is positive (M10). Its outsider result needs the bounds of M6 as well as the
threshold (M11). A fifth proposition, on partial inclusion, does not follow
from its premises (Appendix~\ref{app:refuted}, R1).

\paragraph{Two groups.} The law of motion of one group does not take any
other group's standing, so the independence of the outsider and the host in
M11 is a property of the map rather than a result about how groups interact.
A channel between groups, such as the host's standing entering the
outsider's discrimination rate, would need a row of its own.

\paragraph{The intensity parameter.} The predecessor introduces
$\rho\in[0,1]$ as a discrimination-intensity or rigidity parameter and
places the Coate--Loury self-confirming equilibrium at $\rho>\rho^\ast$. In
its equation (loury-rho), $\rho$ multiplies $s_g\delta$ and appears nowhere
else, so the rows cannot tell it from salience or from the cost of
exclusion. Row M13 states the threshold as a rescaling of M10. A reading of
$\rho$ as a property of belief updating would need a row in which it enters
through the updating rule, and no such row exists.

\paragraph{The predecessor's numbers.} Two of the predecessor's numbers are
confirmed and two are not carried. Its bifurcation figure's
$\delta^\ast\approx0.29$ at $\mu=\tilde\gamma=0.05$ and $s_g=1$ is confirmed,
since M10 puts $b^\ast$ there between $0.29$ and $0.30$, and both panels of
its composed-map figure are confirmed by M17. Its intensity proposition
reports $\rho^\ast\approx0.31$ after the words ``Under base parameters'' and
does not state the parameters, whereas at the parameters of its composed-map figure, panel (a), M17
puts $\rho^\ast$ between $0.58$ and $0.60$. Its empirical prediction that
raising mobility by $0.01$ above the threshold takes about 65 generations
names no parameters either, and M16 bounds the escape time below by
$1/(\sqrt b\sqrt\varepsilon)$, which at $\varepsilon=0.01$ is $10/\sqrt b$.

\paragraph{Passing.} Row M14 carries the composition claim of the
predecessor's passing appendix for a finite population. Row M14 does not carry
the passing decision itself, which the predecessor derives from a benefit
and a cost of suppressing markers that no row defines. Part (ii) says only
what the decision needs to make the leavers an upper set. The feedback from
the lower visible mean to the next generation's standing is the composed map
of M4 to M6, applied to the visible group, and is not restated.

\paragraph{The platform channel.} Row M15 takes the predecessor's
first-order condition as the platform's objective, linear in the admission
rate, and its spillover formula as a definition. The atmosphere vector, the
self-selection function, the willingness to pay and the composition
externality are not derived, and a row that derives them would replace M15.
The predecessor's shadow-market proposition rests on an informed and an
uninformed social quality, $S^\ast$ and $S^{\mathrm{rand}}$, that no equation
of the predecessor defines, so it is not a row. Its partial-inclusion
proposition is Appendix~\ref{app:refuted}, R1.

\paragraph{The group prior across generations.} Each generation the
observer's class distribution for a group is re-learned from the group's
current standing, a choice the author made on 14 October 2026, because this
is the model the predecessor manuscript writes down in its equations (prior),
(dynamic) and (composed) and its loop figure. The alternative, a distribution
frozen at its first value, enters as rows M7 and M8 rather than being set
aside, and conclusion C7 compares the two.

\section{Refuted conjectures}
\label{app:refuted}

\paragraph{R1. The admission threshold lies below the tipping point.} The
predecessor's partial-inclusion proposition states
$c_E<c_M$, and argues that the two thresholds ``answer to structurally
different objects''. That does not order them. With $\bar c_I=1$, for every
$c_M<1$, every $N(1-\alpha)>0$ and every $|A|>0$, there is a willingness to
pay $W>0$ with $c_E>c_M$ and one with $c_E<c_M$
(\lean{Platform.tokenism\_not\_implied}). The claim therefore does not follow
from the premises the predecessor states. A bound on $W$ relative to the
