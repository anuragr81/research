\noindent The model has a static part and a dynamic part. In the static part
an observer meets a person, reads a marker, and forms a belief about the
person's class through the group the marker signals (M1 to M3). In the
dynamic part a group's standing, the mean class of its members, moves from
one generation to the next under three forces, exclusion, convergence and
mobility, and the exclusion force depends on the standing itself through the
discrimination rate (M4 to M12). The discrete objects are the group, the
marker, and in the Lean rows the class. The continuous objects are the
standing, the beliefs, the rates and the maps. Every belief is the observer's
and is a construct attributed to the observer to rationalise what the
observer does. Every rate is a fact of the world that records can reach. The
model contains no payoff and no utility, so no object needs to be read up to
an affine transformation. The engagement decision of the predecessor
manuscript, which turns a belief into a refusal, is not a row yet, and the
discrimination-rate function $F$ stands in for it. Objects that no row
carries are listed with that said, so that their names are fixed before the
rows reach them.

\mm{$\beta_0$}{Frozen prior}{A real number in $[0,1]$.}{The standing imputed

\section*{Structure}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Entering only through others.} $\gamma$ and $\nu$ enter only
  through $\tilde\gamma$. $s_g$ and $\delta$ enter rows M5 to M12 only through
  $b=s_g\delta$, and $F$ enters M7 and M8 only through $F(\beta_0)$. The
  likelihood enters M2 and M3 only through group sufficiency.
\item \emph{Given directly by recorded comparisons.} The direction of a
  standing path. The divergence of paths from different starts (the trap
  condition). The direction of $\tilde\gamma^\ast$ in salience, across groups
  that differ in the marker alone. The host's path against the outsider's
  presence.
\item \emph{Needing a computed reference.} $c_L$, $c_M$, the merged root,
  $\tilde\gamma^\ast$, $b^\ast$ and $c^\ast$, each from the parameters by a
  closed form of M5, M7, M9 or M10.
\item \emph{Elicited and not recorded.} Every belief, the prior, the group
  weights, the three posteriors, the salience and the frozen prior. Each is
  the observer's and rationalises the observer's refusals, which are what the
  discrimination rate records.
\item \emph{Assumed.} The canonical form of $F$ and group sufficiency.
\item \emph{Never observed.} The cost of exclusion on its own, and the
  engagement threshold.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Recovery formulas}

Each formula is a theorem of \texttt{lean/mathlib/MeasurementMap.lean}. The names in
brackets are its theorems.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Convergence rate and mobility, from a group with no exclusion}
  (\lean{MeasurementMap.recovery\_mu\_gamma}). With $s_g=0$ the map is
  $\mathcal{H}(\bar c)=\bar c+(1-\bar c)(\mu+\tilde\gamma\bar c)$. From two
  recorded pairs of consecutive standings $(\bar c_1,\bar c_1')$ and
  $(\bar c_2,\bar c_2')$ with $\bar c_i<1$ and $\bar c_1\neq\bar c_2$, put
  $y_i=(\bar c_i'-\bar c_i)/(1-\bar c_i)$. Then
  \[
  \tilde\gamma=\frac{y_1-y_2}{\bar c_1-\bar c_2},
  \qquad
  \mu=y_1-\tilde\gamma\,\bar c_1 .
  \]
\item \emph{Exclusion pressure, from a group under the canonical $F$}
  (\lean{MeasurementMap.recovery\_b}). With $\mu$ and $\tilde\gamma$ in hand
  and one recorded pair $(\bar c,\bar c')$ with $0<\bar c<1$,
  \[
  b=\frac{(1-\bar c)(\mu+\tilde\gamma\bar c)-(\bar c'-\bar c)}{\bar c(1-\bar c)^2}.
  \]
  The control (\lean{MeasurementMap.control\_recovery\_b\_interior}) shows the
  formula returns $0$ at $\bar c=0$ whatever $b$ is.
\item \emph{Cost of exclusion, from a recorded discrimination rate}
  (\lean{MeasurementMap.recovery\_delta}). With $\mu$ and $\tilde\gamma$ in
  hand, a recorded pair $(\bar c,\bar c')$ and the recorded rate $\phi$ of that
  generation, with $\phi\bar c\neq0$,
  \[
  \delta=\frac{(1-\bar c)(\mu+\tilde\gamma\bar c)-(\bar c'-\bar c)}{\phi\,\bar c}.
  \]
  This separates $\delta$ from $s_g$ only because $\phi$ is recorded.
\item \emph{The discrimination-rate function, pointwise}
  (\lean{MeasurementMap.recovery\_F}). With $s_g\neq0$ elicited and $\phi$
  recorded at standing $\bar c$, $F(\bar c)=\phi/s_g$. One generation gives
  one point of $F$, and its canonical form is a hypothesis about the points.
\end{enumerate}
