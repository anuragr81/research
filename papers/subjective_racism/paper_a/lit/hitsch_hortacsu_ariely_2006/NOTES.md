## The bearing on Paper A1

**A finding that cuts against A1 comes first.** A1's contribution 4 predicts
that the group penalty in contact vanishes when class is shown. On this site
income and education are shown in every profile (HHA-Q2), and the ethnic
penalty in first contacts is measured with them held fixed (HHA-Q3). It does
not vanish. Black and Hispanic men receive about half as many first contacts
from white women as white men do, and Asian men fewer than a quarter
(HHA-Q4). In the logit the penalty is worth $154,000 of a man's income
(HHA-Q8), and for women no income offsets it (HHA-Q9). If class is income and
education, the source rejects contribution 4 for first contacts in dating.

Four qualifications limit how far that rejection reaches. A1 must state
whichever it relies on, and none is checked here.
- Income is self-reported, and 35 percent of men and 47 percent of women do not
  report it (PDF p. 22). A displayed income that viewers do not believe would
  leave the marker informative about class.
- Class in A1 may be wider than income and education. McPherson,
  Smith-Lovin and Cook read educational homophily as cultural rather than
  economic (their MSC-Q11). Fisman et al., as reported here, find that the race
  penalty in speed dating shrinks once "shared interest" is measured (HHA-Q13,
  [C]).
- The setting is mate choice. A1's contact is wider, and the race penalty in
  marriage may carry more than class.
- The paper's race term `ϑ^{kl}` enters utility separately from the attributes
  (eq. 5). It is not identified apart from any trait that is unseen and
  correlated with race, which is the channel A1 proposes.

The candidate contributions of A1 are taken in turn.

1. **Group avoidance from class homophily under inference.** Not in the
   source. The paper's utility has preferences that depend on one's own
   attribute through the distance terms, squared in the logit (HHA-Q11). With
   symmetric weights that is A1's `−κ (c_i − c_j)²` in an attribute that is
   seen (`symmetric_quadratic`). Race enters as a separate taste `ϑ^{kl}`, not
   as an inference about an unseen attribute, and no group's spread is
   penalised. The source is a precedent for an engagement utility that depends
   on one's own type, estimated, and for its failure of assumption 1
   (`control_distance_terms_break_assumption1`, HHA-Q10).
2. **Budget compatibility.** Not in the source, and the evidence on income runs
   against a homophilous preference over income. The distance coefficients on
   income are near zero and "own income matters only slightly" (HHA-Q5).
   Preferences over education do have a homophilous part (HHA-Q6, HHA-Q7). A1
   should say whether budget compatibility predicts homophily in income, which
   this source does not find in mate choice, or in something else.
3. **Venue exclusion.** Not in the source. A footnote names "upscale" and
   "dive" bars and church socials as institutions that "may well have arisen
   endogenously to facilitate sorting" (HHA-Q12). It is not modelled.
4. **A test of the class channel.** The source runs it, for first contacts in
   dating, and the penalty survives (HHA-Q3, HHA-Q4), with the qualifications
   above. Stated and revealed preferences over race also differ (HHA-Q15), so a
   test of A1's channel has to use behaviour.

What A1 should cite the source for. The threshold rule for contact (HHA-Q1),
the estimated squared distance utility (HHA-Q11), the survival of the racial
penalty given income and education in dating (HHA-Q3, HHA-Q4) as the evidence
A1's contribution 4 must answer, and the contrast between vertical income and
homophilous education (HHA-Q5, HHA-Q6).
