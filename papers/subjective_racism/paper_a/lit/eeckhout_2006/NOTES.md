## The bearing on Paper A1

Nothing in the source contradicts the A1 model. The source has no unseen
attribute, so it is a contrast to A1's inference channel rather than a
predecessor of it.

1. **Contribution 1 (avoidance derived from class homophily under
   inference).** Not in the source. Agents are identical apart from colour
   (EE-Q5, EE-Q7), colour is observed (EE-Q8), and nothing is inferred from
   it. Treatment does depend on the observer's own colour, since strategies
   condition on whether the partner shares it (EE-Q10), and exclusive
   segregation ends all cooperation in mixed matches (EE-Q11). That
   dependence is an equilibrium use of colour as a coordination device
   (EE-Q1, EE-Q9), and any public randomisation device would serve (EE-Q6).
   It is selected among equilibria, not derived from a preference over the
   partner. The source has no variance term, because partners differ in
   nothing a payoff depends on. The author sets heterogeneity aside (EE-Q2)
   and names heterogeneity as in Burdett and Coles (1997) as an extension
   (EE-Q14). A1's unseen class is such heterogeneity, and A1 can cite EE-Q14
   for the gap it fills. Burdett and Coles (1997) is not read, so whether
   it already has class inferred from a marker is open.
2. **Contribution 2 (budget compatibility).** Not in the source. Payoffs
   come from a prisoner's dilemma with no budgets or incomes.
3. **Contribution 3 (venues and price screening).** Not in the source.
   Meetings are uniformly random. Directed search by colour is named as
   future research (EE-Q13) and not modelled. There are no prices.
4. **Contribution 4 (the group penalty vanishes when class is shown).** Not
   in the source. In the source's channel there is no attribute to show, and
   segregation persists whatever is known about a partner beyond colour.
   A1's test would therefore separate the two channels. Under A1 the
   penalty vanishes when class is shown, and under the coordination channel
   it does not.
5. **Equilibrium condition on groups.** The source has a consistency
   condition on group composition, not on group means. Under exclusive
   segregation the white share among singles `Π` must be the stationary
   share generated by the strategies (A.6, `singles_quadratic`). This is
   the nearest analogue to A1's requirement that imputed standing equal the
   actual mean of the visible members, and it differs in kind, because it
   fixes who is in the pool rather than what an observer believes about
   them.
6. **Group size.** The source finds that the minority loses under exclusive
   segregation when the majority's share among singles exceeds
   `1/(1 + δ)` (EE-Q12, `minority_worse`), and contrasts this with
   statistical discrimination, where it says size does not matter (EE-Q3).
   If A1's equilibrium makes avoidance depend on group shares, EE-Q3 is a
   statement to address. Whether it does is a question for A1 and not
   settled here.
