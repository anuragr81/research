## The bearing on Paper A1

One finding lies outside A1 as described, and it comes first. A milder accent
within the same accent group is rated better (ST-Q3). A1's marker `g` is a
group label read through the group's class distribution. A graded marker,
whose strength a person can lower and whose strength carries information
within a group, falls outside group sufficiency when strength is correlated
with class within the group. The testimonials also show the same accent read
as middle class in one population and working class in another (ST-Q11),
which in A1 is a difference in the group distribution held by different
observers or venues.

1. **Group avoidance derived from class homophily.** The report has no model.
   Its testimonials describe A1's chain exactly, with class read from accent
   and contact then declined (ST-Q10, ST-Q8), and contact sorted by accent
   (ST-Q2). Mockery in social settings is reported by 55% of senior managers
   of lower parental grade against 34% of higher (ST-Q13), larger than the
   workplace gap of 29% against 22%. That pattern fits A1's claim that the
   penalty bites in contact more than in formal evaluation, but it is a
   report of the target's experience and not of the observer's choice. The
   report has no variance term and does not condition on the observer's
   class. Contribution 1 is not in the report.
2. **Budget compatibility.** Not in the report.
3. **Venue exclusion and price screening.** Not in the report. The elite
   university, the independent school and the professions appear as settings
   in which the accent is heard as out of place (ST-Q6, ST-Q11), but nothing
   is built from client inference.
4. **A test of the class channel.** Not in the report. Expert knowledge
   narrows the accent penalty in ratings of competence (ST-Q15), which shows
   competence, not class.

**Passing.** This is the report's main use to A1. Modification is reported
on entry to settings of higher class (ST-Q6), is motivated by class inference
(ST-Q7) and by competence inference (ST-Q9), carries a cognitive cost and a
cost in one's own group (ST-Q4), can be detected as lack of confidence (ST-Q5),
and falls on speakers of non-standard accents (ST-Q14). The authors' claim that
older professionals modified more (ST-Q12) is an inference that the age figure
does not identify (`control_not_identified`). A1 can cite the report for the
existence and costs of accent passing, but has no number from it on who passes.
