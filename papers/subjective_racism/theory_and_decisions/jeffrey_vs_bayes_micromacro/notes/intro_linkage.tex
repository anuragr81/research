\section{Findings}\label{sec:findings}

\paragraph{Introduction paragraphs thinly expanded.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Gap.} ``Remains valid for any number of cues and attributes'' rests on Lemma SEP alone; no other result is shown beyond two cues.
\item \textbf{Literature.} It previews only the Jeffrey and belief-adjustment strand, while most of Section 3 is about economics.
\item \textbf{Precision.} Its one body home of substance is the paragraph in Section 5 on detectability as a property of the instrument.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Body content with no introduction home.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Three strands of Section 3}: observational learning, decision-theory axiomatisations, and statistical discrimination with audits. The introduction never says the paper positions itself against economics.
\item \textbf{The odds-ratio invariance}, the one statistic that never registers the sequence.
\item \textbf{The homogeneous-prior scope limit} in Section 6.
\item \textbf{The three-coordinates rationale} in Section 4's opening. The current introduction carries it in paragraph 3, but the planned 3A and 3B do not, so it loses its home once they go in.
\item \textbf{The decision model itself} ($v$, $\tau$, the score). The introduction mentions decisions only through their results.
\end{itemize}
