(Table~\ref{tab:names}). Every unit of the body is then tagged with the
introduction paragraphs it expands (Table~\ref{tab:map}). An introduction
paragraph with few body units behind it is thinly expanded, and a body unit
tagged \none\ is content the introduction does not announce
(Section~\ref{sec:findings}). The introduction is taken in its planned form,
with paragraph 3 split into 3A and 3B, and the roadmap is left out. The jobs
are drafted from the headline below, so that the paragraphs read in order
compact into it.

\paragraph{Headline.} The introduction's paragraphs compacted into one sentence, in their order.
\begin{quote}
Sequence effects on judgment are well established but not how far they can be measured, and
Hawthorne leaves open whether evaluators adopt each impression in full or adjust their belief by
a factor; the paper does not resolve that question but measures how full adoption departs from
the sequence-free factor reading of the same impressions, a departure that exists only when the
attributes are believed related and that, for panels reading a credential and a letter in
opposite sequences, is first order in the marginals and the share of decisions changed but
second order in the believed association and the surplus lost; so at a given precision the
sequence-free reading is indistinguishable from sequence-dependent full adoption through some
questions and distinguishable through others, and the paper says exactly which smooth
statistics of the average belief fall on each side.
\end{quote}

\section{Report and proposed remedies}\label{sec:findings}

Each item gives what the mapping shows, a remedy with the paragraph that would host it, and the
grounds for any claim a draft adds. Drafts follow \texttt{notes/writing\_discipline.md}. Nothing
here has been applied to the manuscript.

\subsection*{Introduction paragraphs thinly expanded}

\paragraph{1. Gap: ``any number of cues and attributes''.}
\emph{Finding.} The planned Gap paragraph states the problem only, so the current paragraph 1's
last sentence needs another home. It also claims more than the paper shows: ``Despite its
simplicity the two-cue setting demonstrates the central mechanism of how an attribute-local
updating leaves the interactions among attributes untouched remains valid for any number of cues
and attributes.'' Only Lemma SEP is shown for more than two cues; every order result is proved for
two.
\emph{Remedy (Vignette).} Cut the sentence from paragraph 1, and narrow the vignette's clause
``While the mechanism can extend to higher number of cues'' to what Lemma SEP proves:
\begin{quote}
The setting has two cues, but its mechanism, that an update on one attribute rescales the
association rather than creating it, holds for any number of cues and attributes
(Lemma~SEP).
\end{quote}
\emph{Grounds.} LemmaSEP.lean, general $N$: \texttt{applySteps\_no\_pair\_interaction},
\texttt{sep\_rescales\_association}.

\paragraph{2. Literature: only the Jeffrey and belief-adjustment strand.}
\emph{Finding.} Paragraph 2 previews the commutation debate, belief adjustment and Hawthorne's
factor models. Most of Section 3 positions the paper against economics (observational learning,
decision-theory axiomatisations, statistical discrimination and audits), and the introduction
never says so.
\emph{Remedy (Literature).} One sentence at the end of paragraph 2:
\begin{quote}
In economics, sequence has been studied across agents, as in observational learning
\citep{BHW1992,Banerjee1992}, and discrimination through evaluators' beliefs and preferences
about groups \citep{Phelps1972,Arrow1973,Bohren2019}, while decision theory axiomatises updating
on a single piece of news \citep{Epstein2006,Ortoleva2012}, and none of these asks how one
evaluator's reading sequence registers in what an audit measures.
\end{quote}
\emph{Grounds.} Each description is Section 3's own; the last clause is the paper's positioning
claim, as Section 3 states it (a single agent, two cues, a kinematic rather than an equilibrium
object).

\paragraph{3. Precision: one body home, and two sentences with none.}
\emph{Finding.} Paragraph 5's precision argument is developed in one Section 5 paragraph (the
instrument), with mentions in Assumption 1, Section 5.2 and the conclusion. That is enough for a
framing device. But two of its sentences have no body home: ``Thus sequence effect is a
sequential, myopic structure that makes Jeffrey conditioning the coherent response to a delivered
credence'' is never expanded or explained, and the Domotor sentence cites a source that does not
support the mechanism (pending plan entry 1.7).
\emph{Remedy (Precision).} Cut the ``myopic structure'' sentence, and apply entry 1.7 for the
Domotor sentence.

\subsection*{Body content with no introduction home}

\paragraph{4. Section 3's economics strands.} Covered by remedy 2.

\paragraph{5. The odds-ratio invariance.}
\emph{Finding.} Section 5.2 shows the odds ratio is unchanged by every separable reweighting, so
the odds ratio of each evaluator's belief is the same under both sequences and the benchmark, at
any precision. It is the one question that never tells the two rules apart, and the introduction
does not mention it.
\emph{Remedy (Precision).} A clause at the end of paragraph 5's last sentence:
\begin{quote}
\ldots, and the odds ratio of each evaluator's belief does not tell them apart at any precision
(Lemma~SEP).
\end{quote}
\emph{Grounds.} PropDEC.lean \texttt{oddsRatio\_gap\_eq\_zero}; Ladder.lean
\texttt{oddsRatio\_rescale}. The claim is for each evaluator's belief and not for the average
belief, whose odds ratio is a smooth statistic covered by PRO.

\paragraph{6. The homogeneous prior.}
\emph{Finding.} Section 6 states that every evaluator shares one prior $(\alpha,\beta,c)$ and that a
population differing in $c$ is outside the results. The introduction's audit claims are claims
about populations, and they carry this condition silently.
\emph{Remedy (Results).} A qualifier in paragraph 6's PRO sentence: ``for every interior mixture of
reading sequences in a population whose members share one prior, and across an open set of
priors''. The alternative is to leave scope limits to Section 6, which the roadmap points to.

\paragraph{7. The three coordinates of a belief.}
\emph{Finding.} The planned vignette names the four statistics (the prevalence of a trait, the
association, the share of decisions changed, the surplus lost) but not why these four. Section 4
opens with the reason: a belief about two binary attributes has three coordinates, and the
decisions read from it are counted by how many change and what that costs.
\emph{Remedy (Results).} Replace paragraph 6's opening ``The key finding is presented in two
parts.'' with:
\begin{quote}
A belief about two binary attributes has three coordinates, the two marginal probabilities and
the association, and the decisions read from it give two further statistics, the share of
decisions the sequence changes and the surplus that change costs. The key finding comes in two
parts.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{8. The decision model.}
\emph{Finding.} The planned vignette speaks of hiring decisions changed, but the introduction
never says how a belief becomes a decision.
\emph{Remedy (Vignette).} One sentence in paragraph 4, after ``identical evidence can license
different decisions'':
\begin{quote}
The panel hires when the expected value of the candidate under its belief exceeds the value of
its outside option, so a change of belief changes the decision only for candidates near that
threshold.
\end{quote}
\emph{Grounds.} Setup 2.1 (engage if and only if $s(P)\ge\tau$); Decision.lean \texttt{flips\_iff},
\texttt{not\_flips\_of\_pointing\_away}.

\subsection*{Errors found along the way}

\paragraph{9. ``Correlated'' cues.} Assumption 2 reads ``The cues are bundled, correlated, and soft'',
and the abstract ``two correlated soft cues''. In the model the cues are not correlated: each bears
on one attribute, and the correlation is the evaluator's belief that the attributes go together,
$c$. Pending plan entry 2.6 rewrites Assumption 2; the abstract needs the same correction.

\paragraph{10. Paragraph 2 says an observer cannot tell the rules apart.} Its last sentence ends
``it hardly addresses the empirical issue that an observer cannot tell whether impressions replace
prior belief or adjust it''. The headline says the opposite for some questions: at a precision
finer than $c$, the marginals and the share of decisions changed separate full adoption from the
factor reading. The literature leaves the matter open; it does not settle it in the negative.
\emph{Remedy (Literature).}
\begin{quote}
\ldots, it leaves open whether an observer can tell an evaluator who replaces prior belief with
each impression from one who adjusts it.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{11. Paragraph 1 judges the factor rule unlikely.} Its second sentence argues that
sequence-free judgment would mean ``the unlikely situation where experience does not change how
later cues are read at all''. The planned paragraph 3A says the paper does not resolve
Hawthorne's question, and paragraph 1 would then have answered it on plausibility, the ground
the paper sets aside.
\emph{Remedy (Gap).} Cut the sentence. The first sentence's citations already carry that
sequence effects are well established.

\paragraph{12. Setup 2.2 says the benchmark is not a rival rule.} It reads ``The benchmark $\PB$ is
not a rival updating rule but the same two impressions re-expressed''. The headline sets the
factor reading against full adoption as the rule Hawthorne prefers, which is what makes
distinguishing them bear on his question.
\emph{Remedy (Setup 2.2).}
\begin{quote}
The benchmark $\PB$ is the same two impressions re-expressed, each read as a multiplicative factor
relative to the prior marginal.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{13. ``Order'' for reading sequence.} Three places break the paper's own terminology,
two of them in the paragraph that defines it. Paragraph 5: ``For small enough $c$ the two
sequences separate'' should be ``the two orders separate'', and ``structurally blind to arrival
order'' should be ``arrival sequence''. Section 5.2, opening of the aggregate belief statistics:
``whether arrival order matters or not'' should be ``arrival sequence''.

\paragraph{14. Typos and one misstatement in paragraphs 5 and 6.} Paragraph 5: ``bechmark'' for
``benchmark'', and ``\texttt{\`{}\`{}sequence\`{}\`{}}'' closes with backquotes. Paragraph 6:
``while evaluator diverges'' should be ``while each evaluator's belief diverges''; ``and audit's
answer'' should be ``an audit's answer''; ``One consequence, is'' loses its comma. The last
sentence says the belief audits ``may not even be detectable''; what it means is that they may
not detect the stereotype.
