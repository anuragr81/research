\noindent Setting. The prior covariance is $c=0$, so the two attributes are
believed unrelated, and the adoption weight satisfies $0<\omega<1$, so the cue
read second is adopted in part. At $c=0$ the paper's channel, sequence
dependence carried through the prior association, is switched off. What
remains is a second channel, the weakening of whichever cue is read second,
which is at full strength here. Full adoption ($\omega=1$) is the setting of
Sections~4 and~5, where the second channel is absent and the first vanishes at
$c=0$ (Proposition IMM). All quantities below are exact at $c=0$; the
$\bigO(c)$ corrections are in the script and are not the point.

\multicolumn{4}{@{}l}{\emph{Between the two sequences, one evaluator}}\\
\addlinespace[2pt]
1 & marginal probabilities &
$\PJ_{AB}(A{=}1)-\PJ_{BA}(A{=}1)=(1-\omega)(\alpha-q_0)$ \newline
$\PJ_{AB}(B{=}1)-\PJ_{BA}(B{=}1)=-(1-\omega)(\beta-r_0)$ &
The marginals carry the whole effect. Each is the distance the cue would have
moved its marginal, scaled by how much of it was not adopted. Zero only at
$\omega=1$ or when the cue delivers the prior marginal. The mechanism is
position, not association: in $AB$ the letter is the weakened cue, in $BA$ the
credential is. \\
\addlinespace[4pt]
2 & believed association (cross-product; odds ratio, Yule's $Q$) &
$\assoc(\PJ_{AB})-\assoc(\PJ_{BA})=0$ \newline
odds ratio identical, for every $c$ &
Both sequences leave a product table at $c=0$, so the cross-product is zero in
each; the odds ratio equals the prior's because every damped step is a row or
column rescaling (Lemma SEP). The paper's qualitative verdict survives: the
marginals see the sequence, the association does not. What changes is the
order, zeroth instead of first, and with it the relevance of small $c$. \\
\addlinespace[4pt]
3 & the adoption weight &
$\omega=1-\dfrac{\PJ_{AB}(A{=}1)-\PJ_{BA}(A{=}1)}{\alpha-q_0}$ &
Row~1 read backwards. The channel the paper does not study is the channel
Proposition ADJ reads; the identification device lives entirely at this
point. Requires $q_0\neq\alpha$. \\
\addlinespace[4pt]
4 & decision flip and loss &
score gap $=\langle v,\PJ_{AB}-\PJ_{BA}\rangle$, a fixed multiple of row~1,
independent of $c$ &
The share of evaluators whose decision flips is the mass of a band of fixed
width, not $\bigO(c)$, and the surplus-weighted loss is not second order.
\emph{Inferred from linearity of the score; not separately verified.} \\
\addlinespace[6pt]
\multicolumn{4}{@{}l}{\emph{Pooled over a population, against the benchmark, $0<\lambda<1$}}\\
\addlinespace[2pt]
5 & association of the mean belief &
$\assoc(\Pbar_\lambda)-\assoc(\PB)=$ \newline
$-\lambda(1-\lambda)(1-\omega)^2(\alpha-q_0)(\beta-r_0)$ &
The mean belief acquires an association nobody holds. Every evaluator's table
is independent and so is the benchmark's, but the mixture of two product
tables with different marginals is not a product table. A pooled audit of
whether the traits go together returns a nonzero value in a population where
no one believes they do. The sign is fixed by whether the two cues move their
marginals in the same direction relative to the prior. No counterpart at
$\omega=1$, where the mixture at $c=0$ is $q\otimes r$. \\
\addlinespace[4pt]
6 & marginals of the mean belief &
$\Pbar_\lambda(A{=}1)-\PB(A{=}1)=(1-\lambda)(1-\omega)(q_0-\alpha)$ &
Zeroth order. $\PB$ sets both marginals to their delivered credences, so it is
a full-adoption object under the other reading, and the gap from it mixes two
differences, the reading and the adoption weight. No sequence-free reference
exists at interior $\omega$ under either reading: a Bayes-factor update that
scales the second cue's factor by $\omega$ is itself order-dependent at $c=0$
(script, row 5), because Wagner's commutation needs the same factor in either
position. The between-sequence comparison of rows 1--2 needs no reference,
which is one more reason to prefer Proposition ORD's form of the test. \\

\normalsize

\medskip
\noindent One sentence for the paper. Full adoption is the setting in which the
only source of sequence dependence is the one the paper studies, the
cross-attribute effect carried through the prior association, which vanishes
at $c=0$; partial adoption adds a second source, the weakening of whichever
cue is read second, which is present at $c=0$ and so lies outside the
expansion in $c$ on which the results rest.
