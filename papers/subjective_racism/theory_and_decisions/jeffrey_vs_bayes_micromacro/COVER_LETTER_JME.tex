Dear Editors,

I submit the manuscript ``Effects of Reading Sequence on Belief and Decision Statistics
under Jeffrey Conditioning'' for consideration as a research article in the
\emph{Journal of Mathematical Economics}. The paper is single-authored and original, and it
is not under consideration elsewhere.

The intention for this research has been to understand how belief states may depend on the sequence in which evidence arrives. For soft evidence, where Jeffrey conditioning makes beliefs depend on the sequence, the paper goes beyond the question of whether the dependence is acceptable to ask how it registers in the statistics an observer measures.

The paper's central finding is that in a two-attribute setting, with the prior covariance $c$ as the believed link between the
attributes, each evaluator's belief departs from a sequence-free benchmark at first order,
$\mathcal{O}(c)$. Averaged over a population, the marginal probabilities and the share of
decisions the sequence changes stay first order, while the believed association and the
surplus-weighted loss fall to second order, $\mathcal{O}(c^{2})$. A belief audit can therefore pass while hiring decisions
still record the sequence.

I believe the paper fits the journal's aim of giving economic ideas a formal mathematical expression.
Its object is an audit question with economic stakes, whether a hiring panel's beliefs and
decisions record the sequence in which it reads a file.
