## What it argues

Two desiderata on rules for empirical learning cannot both hold under
(Jeffrey) Conditionalization:

- **Confirmational holism.** For any experience and any proposition there is
  a defeater proposition such that the credence adopted on the experience
  should depend on the credence in the defeater (p. 5).
- **Commutativity on experiences** (occasional, partial). In some cases two
  qualitatively similar experiences in reverse order should bear the same on
  some hypotheses (p. 5). Explicitly *not* commutativity on input
  distributions, which Lange (2000) shows is the wrong desideratum:
  reversing input values does not correspond to reversing experiences (pp.
  3--4, the jellybean .1->.8->.9 vs .1->.9->.8).

Route to the dilemma (§4): the **inputs problem** (p. 10) is what partition
and what values an experience should fix; Field's answer is a Bayes factor
per experience, which is commutative but anti-holistic (only p(E) bears);
**Wagner's theorem** shows Field's is the only commutative answer, so
commutativity on experiences forces the experiential support to be immune to
background beliefs (pp. 12--13).

Diagnosis (§5): **rigidity** (holding conditionals on the input partition
fixed) is itself anti-holistic. *Rigidity Preserves Independence* (p. 16): if
p(E|F)=p(E) and the update is rigid on {E, not-E}, then q(E|F)=q(E). Hence a
later discovery F cannot *undercut* the support E gave to E; it can only
*rebut*, i.e. be evidence against E in its own right. Commutativity merely
makes this time-symmetric.

Conclusion (§6): conditionalization epistemologies are "foundationalism with
uncertain foundations"; either give up holism or give up conditionalization.
