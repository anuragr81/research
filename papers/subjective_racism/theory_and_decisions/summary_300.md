# Discrimination from Soft Signals: Dilemmas of Mimic Men

**Summary**

Anurag Srivastava · Riskcare Ltd., London · University of Reading

---

Social boundaries have persisted in spite of individual counter-examples, legal equality, and formal anti-discrimination instruments. Loury (2002) distinguishes "discrimination in contract" (formal, labour-market) from "discrimination in contact" (informal, social) and argues that the latter sustains racial inequality even after the former is removed. This paper extends Loury to explain how non-discriminatory social environments combined with profit incentives can shape social discrimination through soft signals.

When people encounter someone new, they read soft signals — accent, dress, phenotype, consumption patterns. These markers are outputs of a slow-moving social system (cultural transmission, residential geography, historical experience) and do not update when an individual's economic class changes: an upwardly mobile individual retains the markers of their origin group. The observer cares about the other's economic class, not their group — group enters only as information about class. But a soft marker carries no likelihood linking it to class: an accent does not pin down class, or even group; it merely shifts probability across groups. With no likelihood there is nothing for standard Bayes to condition on, and writing group directly into preferences would assume the discrimination one is trying to explain. What the marker delivers is a shift in credence over groups with the group-level class priors held fixed — the exact evidential form for which Jeffrey conditioning is the unique coherent updating rule. Under it, a high-class member of a stigmatised group is reclassified as an exception while the group stereotype stays put.

The mechanism turns on two governing parameters — marker salience and the group prior, the sediment of economic history — which enter at different points and cannot substitute for one another. Jeffrey updating generates a self-sustaining closure trap as a stable equilibrium, with a mobility threshold γ*, increasing in marker salience, above which the trap is eliminated through a saddle-node bifurcation. As a consequence, anti-discrimination law confined to the contracting channel cannot reach the contact channel of soft signals; only mobility above γ* dislodges the trap.
