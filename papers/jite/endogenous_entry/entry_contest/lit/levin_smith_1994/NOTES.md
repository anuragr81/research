## ERROR FOUND: equation (9) is not a reservation condition

`LITERATURE.tex` says:

> Their equation~(9), `(1-q)^{N-1} V = c`, is the "win only if alone"
> reservation condition and coincides with \citet{FuJiaoLu2015}'s
> Definition~1.

The second half is right; **the first half is wrong**, and the error matters.

Equation (9) appears on p.590 in this derivation. Social welfare in the CV
model with `R^s = 0` is eq. (8):

> `S(q, R^s, e) = [1 - (1-q)^N] V - qNc`

and the paper continues: "with `∂S/∂q = N[(1-q)^{N-1}V − c]`, and
`∂²S/∂q² < 0`. Thus, `e*` must induce entry such that at `q*`, `∂S/∂q`
vanishes: **(9)** `(1-q*)^{N-1}V = c`."

So (9) is the **social planner's first-order condition**, characterising the
socially optimal entry probability — the one the seller's optimal entry fee
`e*` is chosen to induce. It is not a bidder's reservation or indifference
condition. The bidders' equilibrium condition is eq. (1), equivalently
eq. (6), `B_i(q*, Ω) = 0`.

LS-3 verifies the derivation: `dS/dq = N[(1-q)^{N-1}V − c]` exactly, for
`N ∈ {2,3,4,5,8,12}`. Setting it to zero *is* eq. (9).

The paper's own usage confirms it. In the proof of Proposition 8 (p.595) the
quantity is written with a social superscript: "From (9), `(1-q^s_N)^{N-1} =
c/V`; thus `q^s_N` declines with `N`."

**Why this matters rather than being pedantic.** In CV auctions Proposition 3
says free entry is *excessive*: the equilibrium entry probability lies
**above** `q^s`. Describing (9) as the equilibrium reservation condition
therefore identifies the equilibrium with the social optimum and erases the
very wedge Proposition 3 is about. Since `LITERATURE.tex` nominates Prop 3 as
the frame for the deferred welfare section, this is an error sitting directly
under the work that has not been written yet.

**The Fu–Jiao–Lu half is correct.** FJL Definition 1 (p.396) is: `q_0` solves
`(1-q)^{M-1} V - Δ = 0`, and their gloss *is* the win-only-if-alone argument —
"An entrant can secure a payoff `(1-q)^{M-1}V - Δ > 0` from bidding zero when
all other potential bidders stay out". LS-6 confirms the two equations are
algebraically identical under `Δ ↔ c`.

So the honest statement is: **the same equation arises in both papers from
opposite directions** — in FJL as a lower bound on equilibrium entry from a
deviation payoff, in Levin–Smith as the social optimum from a welfare
first-order condition. That coincidence is more interesting than the survey's
version and should replace it.

**Suggested replacement text** for `LITERATURE.tex`:

> Their equation (9), `(1-q)^{N-1}V = c`, is the first-order condition for
> socially optimal entry, obtained by maximising (8); the seller's optimal
> entry fee is set to induce it. It is algebraically identical to
> \citet{FuJiaoLu2015}'s Definition 1, which reaches the same equation from
> the opposite direction, as a lower bound on equilibrium entry derived from
> the win-only-if-alone deviation payoff.

## Second, smaller imprecision: Propositions 8 and 9

`LITERATURE.tex` attributes to "Propositions 8–9" the claim that welfare falls
monotonically as `N` grows beyond `n*`. That is **Proposition 9** alone:

> **PROPOSITION 9:** The level of social welfare generated by optimal auctions
> decreases monotonically as `N` increases beyond `n*`.

**Proposition 8** is a different statement — that the probability of *no
entry* increases as `N` grows beyond `n*` under an optimal mechanism. Both
support the survey's point, but the citation should be to Prop 9 for the
welfare claim, with Prop 8 as the mechanism behind it.

A third, trivial one: the sentence the survey attributes to "footnote 2" —
"The process by which potential bidders divide into these two groups is not
explained" — is **main text** on p.586; footnote 2 is attached to the
preceding sentence and concerns Engelbrecht-Wiggans. Cite it as p.586, fn. 2.

## Upgrade from the reconstruction: LS-I is now a criterion, not an analogy

`RECONSTRUCTION.md` was written before re-reading the survey, and it changed
the status of **LS-I** — the claim that a fixed-prize contest takes Prop 3
rather than Prop 6 as its benchmark.

`LITERATURE.tex` argues this by resemblance: a winner-take-all contest with a
fixed prize is "common-value-like in the relevant sense — the prize does not
grow with entry". True, but it reads as an analogy, and a referee can push on
an analogy.

The derivation gives a criterion instead. The branch is selected by whether
`V_n` varies with `n`. Proposition 6 applies exactly when eq. (18) holds, and
(18) requires the marginal entrant's private gain to equal the increment to
total value. With the prize fixed, `V_n ≡ V`, so `V_n − V_{n−1} = 0`: the
social gain from any entrant beyond the first is **exactly zero** while the
social cost is `c > 0`. The paper says so directly on p.596 — *"In CV
auctions, social gains are zero (and therefore smaller than social costs) for
all `n ≥ 2`"* — a sentence the survey does not cite and which is stronger than
the argument it makes instead.

**LS-7** checks this: under a fixed prize, eq. (18) fails by exactly
`V − W_n`, while it holds exactly in the IPV families of LS-1. So the two
branches are separated by a computation, not a judgement.

**Consequence.** The entry_contest model is in the CV branch **by
construction** — `PROOFS.tex` §Primitives fixes `V` exogenously, which *is*
`V_n ≡ V` — and the welfare section can cite p.596 rather than reasoning by
analogy. It also isolates the single thing that would overturn the verdict: a
prize that grew with participation would make `V_n` increase in `n` and could
reinstate the IPV branch. The model does not do that.
