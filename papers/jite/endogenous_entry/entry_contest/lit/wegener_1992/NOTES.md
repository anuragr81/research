## What it gives the manuscript

The author's use (8 Oct 2026): a survey of the problem, not a problem the
paper solves. The paper's own problem is "the effect of wealth / income
differences on competition for success". Within that use it gives one
concept, cleanly: closure and hierarchy are two different things (WG-1,
WG-5). The model has both and keeps them apart, in sequence:

- **Closure at the gate.** Who may compete is set by wealth through the fee;
  the paying class is an aggregate (WG-8), and its share of the prize is
  what the closure captures.
- **Hierarchy within.** The contest is the hierarchy conception exactly:
  positions open to every entrant and ordered on one dimension (WG-6, and
  M26 for "open").

So the model is not a theory of prestige closure. Its closure is by
property, Weber's class channel, not by honor (WG-3, WG-4), and it does not
supply the mechanism of WG-9. What it does supply is a precise statement of
how a property gate in front of an open hierarchy decides who holds the
positions, which is the class half of Weber's picture.
