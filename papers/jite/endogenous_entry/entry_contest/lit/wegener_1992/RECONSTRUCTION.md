## The argument

1. **A typology (Table 1, p. 256).** Theories of prestige cross the source
   of stratification (order, read as hierarchy; conflict, read as social
   closure) with the orientation of action (normative; rational). The four
   cells found prestige on charisma (order, normative: Shils, Eisenstadt),
   achievement (order, rational: Davis and Moore, Parsons), honor
   (conflict, normative: Weber, Kluth) and esteem (conflict, rational:
   Homans, Blau).
2. **Weber's prestige is closure, not grading (pp. 256-257).** For Weber
   prestige is an attribute of status groups (Stände), not something that
   varies in degree; the order he has in mind "is an order of closure, not
   of hierarchy", and prestige "is not based on achievement, money,
   occupation, productive assets, or authority".
3. **The unsolved problem (pp. 258-261).** No theory merges closure with a
   hierarchy of positions, and Parsons matches the two "by fiat". Prestige
   research should therefore keep two concepts: prestige as a hierarchy of
   positions, and prestige as an attribute of socially closed groups.
4. **The dominant empirical view (pp. 261-266).** Occupational prestige
   scales (NORC, Treiman, Duncan's SEI) assume positions "visible and open
   to everyone" on "a single value dimension"; they show consensus and high
   cross-national and temporal stability, but in status-attainment models
   socioeconomic status outperforms prestige.
5. **Closure in the data (pp. 266-273).** Prestige judgments vary with the
   judge: by gender, and by status. High-status judges polarise the
   hierarchy and low-status judges level it (end-anchoring); Wegener's
   "value consensus paradox" makes this a closure mechanism that "forcefully
   inhibits social mobility" (p. 273).
6. **Conclusion (pp. 273-274).** Study the two concepts separately until the
   mechanism that turns social honor into a hierarchy is found; class theory
   faces the same gap for income.

## Terminology

Footnote 1 (p. 255) reserves "status" for objective differences in assets
and "prestige" for the valuative element. In those terms the model's wealth
is status and its prize is prestige.
