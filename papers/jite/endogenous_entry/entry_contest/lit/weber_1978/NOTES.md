# Notes — Weber (1978), pass 1 (sections read, quotation only)

Run from the repository root `python3 lit/weber_1978/verify_weber.py`. The
suite needs the archive.org text, which it downloads to
`~/.cache/entry_contest/weber_economy_and_society.txt` on first use. The text
is not committed, since the translation is in copyright. No Lean, at the
author's instruction of 8 Oct 2026: the claims are interpretive and are
verified by quotation.

## What supports the architecture

1. **The class channel** (WB-1, p.927). Weber has the distribution of
   property exclude "the non-wealthy from competing for highly valued goods",
   and he says it does so "in accord with the law of marginal utility". The
   model's only wealth channel is the utility cost of a fixed fee, which
   diminishing marginal utility makes heavier for the poor (M23), so entry is
   richest-first (M5, M8).
2. **The separation of class and status** (WB-2, p.306; WB-4, p.932). Money
   "may lead to" status without being a status qualification. In the model
   wealth buys only the chance to compete, and the prize, awarded by rank, is
   valued outside utility of wealth.
3. **Closure** (WB-3 and WB-10, pp.43-44). The fee is a condition on
   participation. M4, the gain falling as more enter, is the interest in
   closure Weber describes.

## Where the model is not Weberian

1. **The contest is anonymous** (WB-5, p.936). Weber gives the property of
   knowing "no personal distinctions" to the market, and makes the status order
   "precisely the reverse". The model's contest scores draws that do not
   depend on who the entrant is (M26), so it allocates the status prize by a
   market-like rule. The Weberian case, standing tied to who one is, is the
   anonymity boundary (M27), where count invariance fails.
2. **No one closes the relationship.** Weber's closure is chosen by
   participants by "monopolistic tactics" (WB-10). The model's fee is
   exogenous. The interest in closure exists (M4), but no agent acts on it.
3. **No status groups.** Weber's status groups claim honour and monopolies
   collectively, with conventions, connubium and commensality (p.306). The
   model's challengers are individuals.
4. **Property becomes status in the long run** (WB-8, p.932). Weber notes the
   link is regular over time. The model is static, so it cannot speak to this.

## What the manuscript may therefore say

A parallel, not a foundation. The model keeps apart a class channel (the cost
of entry) and a status reward (a rank-allocated prize), in the manner of
Weber's distinction, and its fee works like a condition of closure. It does
not model status honour, status groups or chosen closure.
