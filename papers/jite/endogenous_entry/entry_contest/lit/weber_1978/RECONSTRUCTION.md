# Reading of Weber, *Economy and Society* (Roth and Wittich edition)

**Source read.** Max Weber, *Economy and Society: An Outline of Interpretive
Sociology*, edited by Guenther Roth and Claus Wittich, University of
California Press, 1978, read from the archive.org text
(`https://archive.org/stream/MaxWeberEconomyAndSociety/MaxWeberEconomyAndSociety_djvu.txt`,
fetched 8 Oct 2026). The title page and copyright page in that text identify
the 1978 printing. The text is an OCR scan. Page numbers are taken from its
running heads (for example "304 STATUS GROUPS AND CLASSES").

**What was read.** Three sections, in full, chosen because they are where
Weber defines class, status and closure. The rest of the book was not read.

1. Part One, chapter I, §10, "Open and Closed Relationships", pp. 43-44.
2. Part One, chapter IV, "Status Groups and Classes", pp. 302-307.
3. Part Two, chapter IX, §6, "The Distribution of Power within the Political
   Community: Class, Status, Party", pp. 926-937.

**Why this book.** On 8 Oct 2026 the author asked whether the manuscript's
architecture is Weberian and whether the book supports it. The author asked
for a one-line mention in the manuscript and for quotations in the literature
table, and said no Lean is needed. The claims recorded here are claims of
interpretation, verified by quotation.

## The structure Weber gives

1. **Class is market situation.** "Class situation" is "the typical
   probability of procuring goods, gaining a position in life and finding
   inner satisfactions", deriving from control over goods and skills (p.302).
   It is "ultimately market situation" (p.928). The distribution of property,
   "in accord with the law of marginal utility, excludes the non-wealthy from
   competing for highly valued goods" (p.927).
2. **Status is honour, distinct from class.** Status is "an effective claim to
   social esteem" founded on style of life, education and prestige
   (pp.305-306). Money "may lead to" status but is not in itself a status
   qualification (p.306). "Status honor need not necessarily be linked with a
   class situation" (p.932).
3. **The two orders work on opposite principles.** The market "knows no
   personal distinctions" and "knows nothing of honor"; the status order "means
   precisely the reverse" (p.936). Classes are stratified by production and
   acquisition, status groups by consumption and styles of life (p.937).
4. **Closure.** A relationship is "closed" when participation "is excluded,
   limited, or subjected to conditions", and the participants want it closed
   when they expect to improve their position "by monopolistic tactics"
   (pp.43-44).
