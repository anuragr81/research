# Reconstruction of Corcoran and Karels, "Rent-seeking behavior in the long-run"

**Source read.** *Public Choice* 46: 227-246 (1985), the journal pages as page
images, read in full [P]. The PDF is cached at
`~/.cache/entry_contest/corcoran_karels_1985.pdf`, its page images at

**Why this paper.** It is the entry paper that C5, C6 and C13 waited on. It
answers Tullock's objections to Corcoran (1984): what free entry does to a
Tullock lottery when r ≤ 1, and when the count r/(r − 1) is not an integer or
entry causes losses.

## Model

Identical players bet `a` on a lottery that pays X (100 in every example) with
probability a^r/(a^r + Σ b_i^r). The symmetric Cournot-Nash bet is eq. (3),
`a = (n − 1) r X/n²`. Zero profit gives eq. (4), `n = r/(r − 1)`.

## Results

1. **r ≤ 1 (Section 2).** Profit stays positive at every count, so entry never
   stops, and bets shrink towards zero. The paper says splitting one's bet always
   pays. A minimum bet Z fixes the count: everyone bets Z once the Cournot-Nash
   bet falls below it, and entry continues while the share X/n covers Z. The rent
   is used up exactly when X/Z is whole.
2. **r > 1 (Section 3).** Incumbents can bet so that any entrant expects a loss.
   - The range of such "pre-emptory" bets runs from note 8's bet up to X/m.
   - The range narrows as m grows and closes at a count that depends on r
     (Table 3).
3. **Hit and run (Section 4).** Entry happens whenever an entrant could profit.
   - Incumbents settle at Cournot-Nash or collude at the low end of the range.
   - Either way, several counts can be stable equilibria (Tables 4 and 5).
4. **Hardball (Section 5).** Entrants accept short-run losses to force
   accommodation. The count is then the largest m whose range is non-empty
   (Table 6), and aggregate bets come close to X.
5. **Policy (Section 6).** Restrict entry, regulate hardball entry, and use a
   licence fee, which the paper says lowers aggregate bets by its amount.
