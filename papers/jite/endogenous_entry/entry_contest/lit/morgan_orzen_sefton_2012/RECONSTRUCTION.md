# Reconstruction — Morgan, Orzen & Sefton (2012)

**Source.** John Morgan, Henrik Orzen, Martin Sefton, "Endogenous entry in
contests", *Economic Theory* 51 (2012), pp. 435–463. Published version, text
layer present, 29pp.

---

## 1. The model (§3)

`N` identical players, each with endowment `w`, choose between an outside
option paying `F` and entering a Tullock lottery contest for a prize `P`.
A contestant investing `x_i` against total investment `X` earns

> `π_i = w + x_i·P/X − x_i`

**Equilibrium given `n` entrants.** The unique symmetric Nash equilibrium is

> `x_n* = (n−1)P/n²`,  `π_n* = w + P/n²`

so payoffs fall with the number of entrants.

**Equilibrium entry.** Assuming `P > F > P/N²` — so at least one player wants
in, but a contest with all `N` is worse than the outside option — and taking
`√(P/F)` non-integer, `n*` is *"the largest integer such that `P/n*² > F`"*,
giving

> `n* = ⌊√(P/F)⌋`

**The identity problem, in their own words (p.441):** *"since all of the
players are identical in the model, the identity of the players choosing to opt
into the contest is not uniquely determined."*

**Their device (Proposition 1).** Each player draws a private random delay
`d_i` from a CDF with support `[0,ε]`, `ε < 1`, and may enter at any
`t ∈ [d_i, 1]`. Ordering `d_1 < ⋯ < d_N`:

> **Proposition 1.** In the unique PBE, contestants `i = 1,…,n*` enter at time
> `d_i`. All others remain out.

So arrival order, generated by private delays, breaks the tie.

## 2. The experiment (§4–§5)

Six sessions, Nottingham, Spring 2008, 102 subjects; 54 in the small-prize
treatment, 48 in the large. Groups of six, fixed for the session; fifty rounds.
Nine independent groups (small), eight (large).

**Design (Table 1):** `w = 100`, `N = 6`, `F = 10`; `P = 50` (small) or
`P = 200` (large). Predicted entrants `n* = 2` and `4`.

**Observed entry (Table 5):**

| treatment | rounds 1–25 | rounds 26–50 | prediction |
|---|---|---|---|
| small prize | 2.7 | 2.5 | 2 |
| large prize | 3.6 | 3.7 | 4 |

Excess entry in the small-prize treatment is significant in both halves
(p = 0.004, 0.012). The large-prize shortfall differs from Nash at the 6% and
12.5% levels, with no significant change between halves (p = 0.708).

The paper stresses that both deviations run *against* what within-contest
behaviour would suggest: with negative returns to two-person competition one
would expect *fewer* than two entrants in the small treatment, and with
under-investment one would expect *more* than four in the large — *"we observe
precisely the opposite"* in both cases.

**Self-selection by risk attitude, tested and rejected (§5.2.1).** Three
entrants occur frequently in both treatments, which controls for individual
characteristics if risk attitudes are distributed alike. Yet *"in the small
prize three-player contest subjects over-invest and earn less than the outside
option, whereas subjects in the large prize three-player contest under-invest
and earn more"*, with the earnings difference significant at 1%. The same
argument is then applied to a "contest premium" explanation, which also fails
the controlled comparison.

## 3. What could not be reconstructed

2. All the regression tables and randomization tests — none of the statistical
   analysis was re-run, and none of the reported p-values were checked.
3. §2's survey of previous experiments, and the later sections on investment
   dynamics and individual characteristics beyond §5.2.1.

## 4. What this implies for entry_contest

**It is the third independent confirmation of the identity problem**, after
Levin–Smith (fn. 2, mixed entry) and Fu–Lu (fn. 6, sequential entry). MOS use
private random delays. All three devices are properties of the *protocol*, not
of the agents.

P5 is the fourth device and the only one that makes entrant identity a property
of the agents themselves: the wealth ordering pins who enters. MOS-6 makes the
contrast arithmetic — with `N = 6` and `n* ∈ {2,4}` there are `C(6,2) = 15` and
`C(6,4) = 15` distinct equilibrium entrant sets, against exactly one under P5.

**The experiment is not evidence for or against anything in `PROOFS.tex`.** It
holds `w = 100` for every subject by design, so the wealth channel that
generates `k*` is switched off. That is worth stating: the one experimental
paper in this literature deliberately removes the variable our model is about.
