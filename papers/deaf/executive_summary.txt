EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (~1,500 words)
Loss Aversion, Endogenous Reference Points, and
Boom-Bust Asymmetry in Financial Wealth Dynamics
================================================================

MOTIVATION
----------
Financial markets exhibit a well-documented asymmetry between expansions and
contractions. Boom periods — when aggregate dealer wealth sits above its
long-run growth trend — are characterised by rapid balance sheet growth, large
position sizes, and turbulent price movements. Bust periods are slow,
compressed, and grinding. Kindleberger (1978) documents this pattern across two
centuries of financial manias and crashes. Adrian and Shin (2010) show that
dealer balance sheets expand rapidly above funding benchmarks and contract
slowly when limits approach. Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2009) establish that
funding liquidity constraints generate precisely this asymmetry at the position
level. Reinhart and Rogoff (2009), covering 66 countries across eight centuries,
document that the run-up to a financial crisis is associated with high and rising
wealth volatility while the aftermath is characterised by persistent below-trend
deviations with compressed variance. Danielsson and Zigrand (2008) show that
endogenous risk — generated by VaR-constrained intermediaries responding to
their own wealth positions — is higher in expansions than contractions.

The GARCH structure of volatility dynamics has been derived from diverse agent
beliefs in rational belief equilibrium models (Kurz and Motolese, 2001) and from
heterogeneous agent switching (Brock and Hommes, 1998). Bayesian approaches with
regime uncertainty (Veronesi, 1999; Hamilton, 1989) explore the belief channel:
agents learn about regime probabilities and respond differently as posterior
beliefs shift, generating asymmetric volatility through belief dynamics. This
paper explores a complementary channel — the preference channel. Even when
agents are certain about their positions, as dealers in liquid markets are (both
agents observe D_{t-1} relative to the Schumpeterian trend in real time), loss
aversion generates boom-bust asymmetry through the price-setting mechanism.
The contribution is to isolate this channel cleanly and derive its exact
structural consequences: a closed-form variance ratio between expansion and
contraction that is exactly lambda^4 for a measurable preference parameter
lambda, a persistence parameter that is exactly 1/(1+g)^2 for an observable
growth rate g, and orthogonality between the two — so that the source of
asymmetry and the source of persistence are separately identified from different
economic primitives.


WHY THE PREFERENCE CHANNEL, AND WHY NOT FULL CPT
-------------------------------------------------
This paper focuses on liquid, exchange-traded settings where prices are publicly
observable, adverse selection is attenuated, and dealer risk management is the
primary source of price impact asymmetry. In these markets the mechanism
operates on certainty rather than uncertainty: the regime itself is publicly
observable. This is why the preference channel is the right starting point
for these markets, and why the Schumpeterian growth rate enters as a
deterministic mean-reversion rate rather than as an inferred parameter.

Cumulative Prospect Theory has the value function kink and asymmetric loss
weighting that should generate boom-bust dynamics, with extensive experimental
support across cultures. The difficulty is that its probability weighting
function is rank-dependent and non-differentiable, incompatible with recursive
dynamic modelling, and its rational expectations reference point requires an
intractable fixed-point recursion when the reference point and equilibrium
volatility are jointly determined. In the spirit of Koszegi and Rabin (2006,
2007), this paper retains the value function kink and replaces the reference
point with the Schumpeterian wealth trend — publicly known, not endogenised —
in a Stackelberg market. Unlike Barberis, Huang and Santos (2001), who generate
time-varying risk aversion from individual performance histories in a
representative agent consumption CAPM characterised through numerical simulation,
this paper derives exact closed-form structural mappings from fixed dealer loss
aversion against a shared social benchmark in a strategic market equilibrium.

By isolating the preference channel, the paper establishes that loss aversion
alone — without belief dynamics — is sufficient to generate the boom-bust
asymmetry with structurally identified parameters. The belief channel and the
preference channel are complementary objects of study. The interaction of the
two channels is a direction for future work.


THE TWO NECESSARY STRUCTURAL CONDITIONS
----------------------------------------
Two structural conditions are jointly necessary — removing either eliminates a
specific identified result exactly.

The first is the shared reference point combined with zero-sum dynamics and
within-period position closing. Because total detrended wealth is conserved,
the market maker and speculator always sit on opposite sides of the kink
simultaneously — a derived theorem, not an assumption. In expansion the market
maker posts low price impact and the speculator takes large positions: wealth
deviations are wide and volatile, the boom, with variance lambda^4 times the
contraction level. At the Tversky-Kahneman estimate of lambda = 2.25 this is
a factor of approximately 25. In contraction the market maker is protective,
posts high price impact, and the speculator takes small positions: deviations
compress, the bust.

The second is the Schumpeterian growth trend, which pins volatility persistence
to the growth rate through beta = 1/(1+g)^2. At g = 0 the IGARCH boundary is
reached: shocks become permanent. Setting lambda = 1 collapses gamma = 0
exactly — boom and bust variance become equal — confirming loss aversion is
necessary as well as sufficient for the asymmetry.

Loss aversion must reside in the market maker: it cancels in the speculator's
first-order condition and produces no asymmetry there. Li, Petrasek and Tian
(2025), using desk-level regulatory data, document that US Treasury dealers
reduce position changes and require higher compensation as they approach VaR
limits — the contraction regime behaviour of the model.


STRUCTURAL RESULTS
------------------
The paper derives a Threshold-GARCH recursion for the conditional variance of
detrended wealth deviations with exact orthogonal mappings:

  Persistence:  beta = 1/(1+g)^2,  with d(beta)/d(lambda) = 0
  Asymmetry:    |gamma|/alpha = (lambda^4-1)/lambda^4,  with d(|gamma|/alpha)/dg = 0

These are exact structural results verified symbolically. The approximation
enters only in the leading-order expression for beta, with error bounded under
6% at representative parameter values.

The cancellation result delivers a precise statement about news interpretation.
The return innovation has constant variance in both regimes: the same news
arrives with identical statistical properties in expansion and contraction.
What differs is the wealth consequence — lambda^2 times larger in expansion.
Loss aversion distorts the response to news, not its interpretation,
asymmetrically across the Schumpeterian threshold.

The model extends Kyle's (1985) three market quality dimensions into a
regime-switching setting. Depth and tightness are lambda^2 times greater in
expansion than contraction. Resilience — the speed of recovery from a shock —
is beta = 1/(1+g)^2, a dimension Kyle does not address. The deviation recursion
is the structural counterpart of Hasbrouck's (1991) VAR decomposition, with
regime-dependent GARCH innovation variance as the extension beyond Hasbrouck's
constant-variance framework.


THREE STRUCTURAL IMPLICATIONS OF THE SEPARATION PROPERTY
---------------------------------------------------------
The following three implications follow directly from the model's exact
mappings, independent of empirical calibration. They are structural inferences,
not empirical findings. Testing whether beta co-varies with trend TFP growth
and |gamma|/alpha co-varies with experimental loss aversion estimates across
markets — with zero cross-effects — is the paper's primary empirical direction.

A STRUCTURAL ESTIMATOR FOR MARKET-LEVEL LOSS AVERSION.
Once g is measured from trend TFP growth and beta is estimated from the
persistence of dealer wealth volatility, the asymmetry ratio
|gamma|/alpha = (lambda^4-1)/lambda^4 identifies lambda from market data
without auxiliary assumptions. Cross-country variation in the estimated lambda
could be compared to experimental estimates from Wang, Rieger and Hens (2017)
— who provide loss aversion estimates across 53 countries — as an out-of-sample
validation of experimental findings in a market context.

THE IGARCH BOUNDARY AS A STRUCTURAL EARLY WARNING INDICATOR.
As g -> 0, beta -> 1: the qualitative character of wealth volatility changes
from stationary to integrated. This is a mathematical consequence of
beta = 1/(1+g)^2. When estimated persistence approaches one, the model says
trend growth is approaching zero — a structural regime change, not a temporary
shock. This reframes financial stability monitoring: the IGARCH boundary is a
structural indicator of stagnant growth with direct macroeconomic interpretation,
not a statistical nuisance to be modelled around.

STRUCTURAL INDEPENDENCE OF MACROPRUDENTIAL AND GROWTH POLICY.
Because beta = f(g) only and |gamma|/alpha = f(lambda) only, the two policy
optimisation problems are structurally independent. The optimal effective
lambda — set through VaR limits or capital requirements — can be chosen without
affecting the optimal g, and vice versa. Neither instrument substitutes for the
other and neither contaminates the other's structural target. The normative
question of what the optimal lambda and g should be requires a welfare function
the model does not specify; what the model delivers is the structural result
that the two choices are orthogonal by construction.

Reducing effective lambda through macroprudential constraints is a Pareto
improvement within the model: the speculator is unaffected (lambda cancels in
the first-order condition), the market maker is protected from excessive loss
aversion, and the real economy benefits from more stable credit and faster
recovery. The objective is not to eliminate the asymmetry — some boom-bust
asymmetry reflects rational intermediary caution and the high-variance
expansion that enables price discovery — but to moderate excessive asymmetry.


LIMITATIONS
-----------
Three limitations apply. The beta = 1/(1+g)^2 identification is a leading-order
approximation with error bounded under 6% at representative parameter values.
The Schumpeterian reference point abstracts from heterogeneous benchmarks and
time-varying growth — the most tractable extension being time-varying g, which
delivers Markov-Switching Threshold-GARCH with growth cycles as the state
variable. Finally, the structural recursion governs the conditional variance of
detrended wealth deviations, not equity return variance. The cancellation result
establishes that return variance is regime-invariant. The connection between
wealth deviation variance and return variance requires a bridge argument left
for future work.
