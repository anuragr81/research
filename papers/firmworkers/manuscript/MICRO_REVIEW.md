# Review of the source's micro claims against the micro dynamics (pass 1, 12 Oct 2026)

The source states its micro conclusions as a summary ("It follows from the dynamics…", appendix, `inputs/firmworkers_corrected.tex` lines 382–436). With the macro model deferred, each claim is judged here only by what follows from the micro model as decided. Nothing in `claims.yaml` has been changed; the verdicts are proposals for discussion.

## The micro dynamics used

One period, the one-shot comparison Q5 left to tractability. A displaced worker with former income `x_i` and loss `ℓ = x_i − x_0` chooses one of three routes:

| Route | Income | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Downgrade | `x_0` (bottom rung) | M32, reading (a) |
| Return to the former job | `x_i − κ ℓ^b` | M7's cost `(x_i − x_0)^b`, with a scale `κ` (`κ = 1` is issue 1's option (a)) |
| Lottery | `x_k − L`, `k` uniform over the other entrants | M11, M14, M32 |

Results are form-free (any nondecreasing `u`) where marked; otherwise risk-neutral. All Lean is in `lean/IscLean/Routes.lean` (28 theorems, axiom-clean, built and audited by `check.py --lean`), plus M33–M35 in `Displacement.lean`. `Routes.lean` is not yet listed in the manuscript because no ledger row cites it.

## Pass 2 (14 Oct 2026): the steady state

The author's decision (M39): one type of player, the worker, in two states; uniform displacement at rate `d`; the steady state as the object; routes compared by lifetime value `V(x) = (x + βdW)/(1 − β(1 − d))`, which is affine in income with slope the lifetime multiplier `A = 1/(1 − β(1 − d)) ≥ 1` (`SteadyState.lean`, audited). Two consequences for every one-shot result: comparisons that hold for any nondecreasing utility of income carry over unchanged, because `V` rises with income; comparisons of a one-time cost with an income gain change, because the gain is multiplied by `A` while the cost is not (issue 13).

## Mobility test (15 Oct 2026), before deciding issue 12

The author's concern: a fallback rule that closes the path from the bottom to the top builds immobility into the baseline, so closure could not then be analysed as its cause. The test: with no closure and one utility for everyone, can a worker of any rank reach the top and return? `lean/IscLean/Mobility.lean` (audited) answers it in the sharpest case, a pool of exactly two, the top worker and a worker of rank `i`, which uniform displacement produces with positive probability. In that pool the lottery is a sure exchange, so the path is open exactly when both weakly prefer the lottery, and since the exchange is sure, no risk attitude enters.

| Rule | Does the top accept the exchange? | Does rank `i` accept it? | Lean |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bottom rung (M32 as decided) | Yes, with any entrant whose income less `L` is at least `x_0`, provided the top's return is worth no more than `x_0` (the cost bound of M33's top case), for every nondecreasing `u` | Yes, whenever `x_i ≤ x_top − L`, for any upskilling cost and every nondecreasing `u` | `bottom_rung_top_accepts_entrant`, `entrant_accepts_top` |
| Proportional step `(1 − δ) x_top` | No, for every entrant whose income less `L` is below the top's reduced pay, for every strictly increasing `u`. On the Pareto ladder at `a = 2`, `δ = 1/4`, the top at quantile `99/100` refuses every rank up to quantile `8/9` | — | `proportional_top_refuses_entrant`, `control_proportional_refusal_reaches_far_down` |
| Either rule, routes a fixed function of rank | A rank that returns is sealed: nothing reaches it and it reaches nothing, while lottery ranks reach one another in one move | | `return_rank_sealed`, `return_rank_unreachable`, `lottery_ranks_communicate` |

Controls: under the bottom rung a top with a cheap return refuses a low entrant (`control_bottom_rung_needs_cost_bound`); under the proportional step the top accepts an entrant paying at least its reduced pay plus `L` (`control_proportional_top_accepts_near_entrant`).

Reading. The bottom rung keeps the bottom-to-top path open through the lottery itself, which is the channel closure acts on, and it does so for every risk attitude. The proportional step closes it for every rank below the top's reduced pay, which at JLS's `δ ≈ 1/4` is most of the ladder. The third row is the caveat for both rules: a rank whose displaced holder always returns is sealed. With a random pool that caveat weakens, since a rank whose return is worth less than `x_top − L` gambles whenever it is displaced together with the top (`entrant_accepts_top`), so under the bottom rung every rank at least `L` below the top is connected to the top, and through it to every other such rank. Not yet proved: that the resulting chain is irreducible on the whole ladder, which needs the reassignment's positive probabilities (M36) and `0 < d < 1`.

## The structure of the groups under the two-stage rule (17 Oct 2026)

What the two-stage rule (option (a) for M29) describes at the level of the groups rather than the individual entrant. Three parts; the first two are in Lean, the third is a derivation, not yet proved.

1. **What is conserved.** Every reassignment conserves the number of jobs each status group holds (M50, `group_job_count_conserved`). So the state of the groups is *which* ranks each holds, not how many, and a "gap" between the groups is a difference in their rank distributions.
2. **How the state moves.** Only the joint lottery moves jobs across the groups. In the pool of two and two, the expected gap between a group's total and half the pool's total is multiplied each lottery by `ρ(σ) = σ²(2 − σ²) − (1 − σ²)²/3` (M51, `two_stage_gap_recursion`), from `−1/3` at `σ = 0` (the gap reverses and shrinks to a third, because nobody keeps their own job) to `1` at `σ = 1` (the gap is permanent). The chance that the groups' positions are untouched is `(1 − σ²)²/9 + σ²(2 − σ²)` (M49).
3. **The large pool, not in Lean.** With `n_A` and `n_B` entrants and lone lotteries rare, the joint lottery has about `(1 − σ)n_A` and `(1 − σ)n_B` members, and a uniform reassignment of `m` members gives each entrant each other's job with chance `1/(m − 1)`, so the expected number of jobs crossing from `B` to `A` is about `(1 − σ) n_A n_B/(n_A + n_B)`, and the same from `A` to `B` (M50). With uniform displacement at rate `d`, `n_A = dN_A` and `n_B = dN_B`, and the expected gap between the groups' mean ranks decays by the factor `1 − (1 − σ)d` per period, so its half-life is about `ln 2 / ((1 − σ)d)`: infinite at `σ = 1`, `ln 2/d` with no closure. Both `σ` (Coleman's inbreeding homophily index, L24) and `d` (X28) are measurable, so the half-life is a prediction from two measured quantities. The steady state for `σ < 1` is group-blind, by the symmetry of the process under relabelling jobs; closure governs the speed, and at `σ = 1` the gap never closes.

This is the structure (c) cannot describe: per-entrant weights fix nobody's joint behaviour, so neither the persistence chance nor the gap recursion is determined under (c), and unequal pools are outside it.
