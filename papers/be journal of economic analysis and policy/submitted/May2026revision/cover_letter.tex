I write to submit a revised version of the manuscript
``Participation Costs, Social Capital, and the Limits of Mobility
Policy: A Model of Status Competition under Income Inequality''
for consideration in \textit{BEJEAP}. This follows from an earlier version that was 
submitted in October 2025. The current revision
responds directly to the referee's comments.

The paper employs microeconomic theory to analyse when and why
income inequality suppresses participation in status competitions
for elite credentials, and what policy instruments can restore
mobility. The paper situates itself in the positional competition tradition
rather than the human capital tradition - viewing social capital as a
rank-building input into status tournaments rather than
informational. The paper's main finding are a hard mobility ceiling
$\mu/[2(1-\mu)]$ determined by the design of the
competition rather than by who participates in it, and a formal
demonstration that participation-cost reduction and competition
redesign are complementary instruments. The results speak directly to the design of
credential programmes, hiring processes, and access policies.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Response to Referee}

\medskip

Two major issue were identified by the referee: the restriction to
$N=2$ players and the absence of clear propositions. To address these issue, the model places one high-income participant against $Q \geq 1$ low-income challengers and derives all results --- winning
probabilities, Nash equilibrium conditions, the participation
threshold, and the mobility ceiling --- for general $Q$. Formal propositions with proofs have been added for the Case~II equilibrium (Proposition~1) and the asymptotic probability surplus (Proposition~2). A new Section~3 develops the policy
content at length.

The referee also raised four other concerns.

\textit{Zero-sum structure.} The paper is designed for positional
competitions with a fixed number of elite positions ---
partnership tracks, academic appointments, competitive admissions
--- where the zero-sum characterisation is accurate rather than
approximate. A paragraph in the model setup makes this scope
explicit.

\textit{Signaling language.} The referee the paper's mechanism as contest-theoretic rather than signaling-theoretic. All signaling language has thus been removed. The mechanism is now characterised explicitly as a productive
rank-building input in a tournament - since social capital enters the composite score
directly and raises the probability of winning through the
contest scoring rule.

\textit{Gallice and Grillo (2019).} This paper has been added
to the literature review with explicit engagement on two aspects: contest versus signaling mechanism; and the threshold cost structure that generates the binary
equilibrium outcomes and the mobility ceiling.

\textit{Uniform distributions.} A robustness remark has been
added explaining that the key qualitative results --- the binary
equilibrium structure and all comparative statics --- hold for
any pair of symmetric distributions on $[0,1]$. The
closed-form expressions are specific to the uniform case as a
tractable parametric representation of a more general result.
