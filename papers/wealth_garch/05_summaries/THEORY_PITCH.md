# Pitch

Much of finance reads a firm's attitude to risk off its volatility. This paper solves a model of bank capital in which that instinct provably fails, and shows where to look instead.

A bank holds a buffer against a reference level, chooses risk under a regulatory cap, and pays out or recapitalises at two thresholds. Shortfalls are penalised more heavily than equivalent surpluses; set that weight to zero and the problem nests its risk-neutral counterpart exactly.

The deficit-to-surplus variance ratio is a constant of the cap's geometry, carrying no information about the preference. The thresholds carry it, and both derivatives come in closed form: a more shortfall-averse bank holds a larger buffer before paying out and recapitalises earlier. The fixed cost of raising capital moves the trigger the opposite way, and that opposition makes both parameters jointly identified from the two thresholds — non-degenerately, not approximately. Preference lives in the boundaries a firm commits to, not in the volatility it produces.
