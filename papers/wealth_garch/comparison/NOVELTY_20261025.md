## Consequences for the headline

"Bank capital buffers guard the well-capitalised line" is Orozco and
Rubio's finding, not ours. A headline that survives this triage is about
the model: the standard dynamic model of bank capital fails on levels
because it uses the wrong floor and the wrong risk, and a version with
the well-capitalised floor and book-capital risk fits out of sample. Its
novelty rests on three reads still to do: Orozco and Rubio, Lang and
Menno, Gropp and Heider (and Craine and Martin for a minor point).

## Read in full, 25 October 2026: Orozco and Rubio (2025)

Recorded in lit/orozco_rubio_2025 (OR-Q1 to OR-Q13, verified). Three
consequences:

1. Confirmed: the well-capitalised 10% line is the binding floor in
   1996-2009 (OR-Q1, OR-Q3); not ours to claim.
2. Their design is local to the line, 8% to 12% (OR-Q10, OR-Q11); the
   level of buffers far above it, F2, is outside it. The model result
   (a dynamic barrier model with that floor matching the level of
   buffers out of sample) is not in their paper.
3. After 2009 the 10% line stops binding and the conservation-buffer
   floor of 10.5% takes over (OR-Q5, OR-Q6, OR-Q13). That is our R, and
   it gives an independent source for the floor: 10% before 2010, 10.5%
   after. The enhanced model's floor is then sourced rather than chosen
   after a look, but on the test window the source points to R, not to
   the 10% made central in PREREG_comparison_17.md. Any re-scoring with
   a period-dependent floor is a new pre-registration, stating that both
   floors' test-window results have been seen.

Still to read: Lang and Menno (2026), Gropp and Heider (2010; their
regulation finding is answered by OR-Q3 but their bank fixed effects bear
on the constant margin), Craine and Martin (2017).

## Read in full, 25 October 2026: Lang and Menno, Gropp and Heider, Craine and Martin

Recorded in lit/lang_menno_2026 (LM-Q1 to Q12), lit/gropp_heider_2009
(GH09-Q1 to Q16) and lit/craine_martin_2017 (CM-Q1 to Q11), all verified;
OR-Q14 added. Each was read in full by a reading agent of this session and
every quotation checked against its PDF.

| Claim | After the reads |
|---|---|
| Barrier model with the regulatory floor fits buffer levels out of sample | Not in any of the four. Lang and Menno is a two-period representative-bank model with a common shock, calibrated for illustration, never fitted (LM-Q2, Q3, Q6), and has no buffers far above its floor (LM-Q7). Orozco and Rubio is local to 8%-12% (OR-Q10, Q11). Survives. |
| The floor is minimum plus conservation buffer after 2009 | Supported in theory (LM-Q5, LM-Q7) and evidence (OR-Q5, Q6). A source, not ours. |
| Regulation shapes buffer levels | Gropp and Heider say second order (GH09-Q1, Q16), but test against the Basel Tier 1 minimum on large listed banks and find regulation matters at their 6% Tier 1 cutoff, the well-capitalised Tier 1 line (GH09-Q6, Q8, OR-Q14); Orozco and Rubio read their result as the wrong line (OR-Q3). Answerable. |
| Size gradient not through risk or access | Sign known (GH09-Q9, BDF-Q26); their access reading (GH09-Q10) is untested on unlisted banks, which they exclude (GH09-Q4). Our test is new, small. |
| Constant extra margin | Bank fixed effects (GH09-Q12) are the antecedent for bank-specific levels, not for a margin common to all banks. Open. |
| Book capital moves at about a fifth of market equity | Direction known (CM-Q1, Q8); theirs is dispersion across 18 banks at one date (CM-D1). Our size over time across a panel is new, small. |
| Loss-based 1x, market 8x, buffers 3x | Not in any; but Craine and Martin find the stress test and the market call for similar average changes in capital (CM-Q6), so the paper must explain that ours are risk scales, not mean shortfalls. |
| Pre-registered scoring of the models on common data | No competitor found. |

Verdict: the enhancement paper's headline survives the four reads, in its
narrowed form. What is new is the model result (the dynamic barrier model
with the floor regulation makes binding fits buffer levels out of sample,
where the standard model does not), and the scale measurements. That
banks guard the regulatory floor is Orozco and Rubio's, and its theory is
Lang and Menno's; both are cited, not claimed.

## Citation search, 28 October 2026 (C5 to C7 of enhancement/ENHANCEMENT.tex)

A triage agent searched citing works of Peura and Keppo (IDEAS list of 89)
and of Orozco and Rubio, structural models fitted to US banks, bank size and
capital, and book against market risk (abstracts and landing pages only;
Google Scholar, SSRN and ScienceDirect refused access). Two items were
already in the bundle and were checked in full against their PDFs.

- Peura and Keppo (PK-Q35 to Q38, added). They solve for the volatility that
  reproduces observed buffers, 1.06% against an estimate of 0.82% for large
  banks and 1.19% against 1.06% for small (multiples about 1.3 and 1.1), on
  their restricted 1983-2002 subsample, and find larger banks hold less
  capital beyond volatility, which they assign to capital-market access.
  The implied-volatility idea is theirs; C6 narrows to its size on common
  data and recent windows (about 3) and its comparison with loss-based and
  market measures. Their access reading of the size gradient is what
  results_access.md does not support.
- Dai, Huang and Keppo, in Huang's thesis (DHK-Q15 to Q20, added). Two
  versions of their dynamic model are tested out of sample, 2004-2015, on US
  banks, scored on equity value, dividends and recapitalisations; accounting
  smoothing hides about a third of asset volatility, and the larger volatility
  brings the dividend barrier closer to the observed level. C5 narrows to
  scoring the published models of different authors against each other on
  pre-registered buffer facts; their hidden volatility is the nearest
  antecedent of C6 and of E8.
- To read in full, not in the bundle or on Drive: Juselius, Ponte Marques and
  Tarashev (2025, BIS WP 1287), management-buffer targets against the
  volatility of buffer shocks for US and euro-area banks (C6); Sengupta and
  Hogue (2014, Kansas City Fed Economic Review), stand-alone banks hold more
  capital (C7). Both publishers refused scripted downloads.
- Medium, for positioning: Corbae and D'Erasmo (2021, Econometrica), large
  banks hold smaller buffers through more diverse funding (C7); Migueis
  (2017, FEDS), a recalibration of Peura and Keppo; Acharya, Engle and
  Pierret (2014), stress-test against market losses; Kovner, White and Zhou

  Ashcraft (2008), holding-company support. Their abstracts only were seen.
