## Primitives

- Holding companies: 281 publicly traded bank holding companies, 237 of
  them multibank, Federal Reserve Y-9 data, annual (p. 141).
- Subsidiaries: 2001 banks of the 237 multibank holding companies, Call
  Reports, 1986-1989, 4398 observations (p. 141).
- Loan growth: the percentage change in loans; internal additions to
  capital: net income before extraordinary items plus loan loss provisions,
  scaled by lagged loans (p. 142).
- Surplus capital: the leverage-based capital ratio less the required
  ratio, 5.5% in 1981-1984 and 6% in 1985-1989; BIND is one if surplus is
  not positive (p. 142).

## Derivation chain

1. Capital market frictions arise at the holding company, which accesses
   the market (p. 138).
2. Regulation impedes consolidated capital management: the building-block
   approach, the source-of-strength doctrine and Sections 23A and 23B
   (pp. 139-140). The supervision manual describes parents that keep their
   subsidiaries at minimum capital by upstreaming excess capital as
   dividends (p. 139).
3. Holding-company regressions (Tables 3 to 5): loan growth rises with
   internal additions to capital, more steeply near the minimum; the
   sensitivity is $4.53-34.95\,s$ in surplus $s$ (p. 148).
4. Subsidiary regressions (Tables 6 to 8): loan growth responds more to
   the rest of the holding company's cash flow than to the bank's own, and
   to the holding company's binding requirement rather than the bank's
   (pp. 153, 156).
5. Appendix: underwriting fees from a Heckman two-step model; high-fee
   holding companies are more cash-flow sensitive (pp. 152, 160-163).

## Roles in this bundle

- Evidence that capital is managed at the holding company: the
  subsidiary's own capital position is not what binds (HJM-Q8).
- The supervision manual's description of upstreaming excess capital as
  dividends (HJM-Q1) is a mechanism by which a subsidiary pays dividends
  while near its minimum.
