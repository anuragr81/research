\section{Scope}

The map names every object of the model of the theory paper once, and the
manuscript uses these names and no others. The names are those of the parent
bundle's \path{MEASUREMENT_MAP.tex}, agreed with the author on 10, 13 and 19
October 2026, and four names the author accepted on 23 October 2026, the
solvency floor, the run-off rate, the liquidity haircut and the value function.
Inputs are what the model takes as given, internal objects are built from the
inputs inside the model, and outputs are what the rows predict. The column
Directly observed says Yes when a record of the world gives the object without
any computation, which is when its last column begins with Recorded, and No
otherwise. The other status words are Elicited, Computed, Assumed, Never
observed and A device. Every symbol in the manuscript's table of assumptions
has exactly one row here. Where a row names the panel, it is the FDIC panel of
US banks that the parent bundle read, 2015 to 2024, and the figures and the
row numbers G1 to G22 are those of the parent map. Lean names are theorems of
\path{theory/lean/}, audited by \path{verify_theory.py}.

The theory paper is a theory of what a bank's policy would reveal if the bank
followed a threshold policy, and the map says what that leaves for
measurement. The asymmetry parameter and the fixed issuance cost are never
observed. The two thresholds that identify them are recorded as the state at a
dividend and at an issue, and on the panel read neither behaves as a
threshold. The limits of buffer variance are computed from assumed volatilities
and apply where the cap binds, and on that panel it does not. What reaches the
data is the bound of M12 and the two recorded thresholds as events.
