## Pass 2, 28 Oct 2026: first findings

F1 (GAP, K1, C1, C4, C6). The economic content of M1 to M3 is in hypotheses
that Lean does not carry: the verification theorem, the envelope identities,
C^2/C^3 regularity, and the signs W(y*)>0, N(y*)>0, N'<0, W'(x_L)>0,
V''(y_post)<0, Psi(x_L)>0, rho_L>mu'(y*). Lean proves the algebra that turns
these into dy*/dtheta, dx_L/dtheta and the sign of the determinant. The
manuscript says so (Appendix B, K1 scope note). The signs are shown at one
parameter point and at 42 of 56 grid points by the solve. A referee will read
M1 to M3 as the implicit function theorem applied under assumed signs.

F2 (ISSUE to confirm, K2, C2, C3, M11). Under the optimal policy the state is
reflected at y* and jumps from x_L to y_post, so when the trigger is interior
the state stays in [x_L, y*]. The limits of M11 are taken as x -> 1+ and
x -> infinity, which the controlled state does not approach. The deficit
limit is exact on (1, xbar] (M10), so it is attained on [x_L, xbar] when the
solvency branch binds there. The surplus limit is never attained: on the
liquidity branch zeta(x) is far above nu_3^2 at x = y*. "The optimal exposure
binds the cap deep in surplus" has no content above y*, where the policy is an
immediate dividend. To be confirmed against the solve (Pass 4).

F3 (GAP, K2). Where the cap binds, the exposure is pinned by the cap, so
buffer variance is a function of (sigma, sigma_L, c, a_i) and of no other
parameter. The absence of lambda_S from the limits is therefore not specific
to lambda_S: K, kappa, rho_L, mu are absent too. The content is that a binding
cap pins variance, which is the geometry of L1 and L5.

F4 (OPEN). Appendix B says W'(x_L)>0 "holds exactly when
rho_L W(y_post) < R - x_L". From the ODE of W at x_L+ this needs a condition
on W''(x_L+) as well. Check the Lean (Envelope.lean) for what is proved.

F5 (OPEN). Which cap the solve uses. If exposure is at the cap on 85 percent
of the inaction region on the leveraged cap, the solve's bank holds risky
assets of several times its assets near y*, the case C3 calls the
mathematical benchmark rather than the banking case.

## Pass 3 and 4, first results, 28 Oct 2026

F2 CONFIRMED for the solve's own parameters (cap of the parent baseline,
a1 = 0.045, a2 = 0.05, a3 = 0.30, sigma = 0.08, sigma_L = 0.03, c = 0.2).
Buffer variance on the cap, zeta(x) = Q(omega), omega = u(x)/(x-1):

| x | pi | zeta | zeta / nu_3^2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.026 (x_L at the base point) | 0.56 | 3.140 = nu_1^2 | 45.6 |
| 1.168 (xbar) | 3.19 | 3.140 | 45.6 |
| 1.55 (y_post) | 3.23 | 0.521 | 7.6 |
| 1.74 (y*) | 3.24 | 0.365 | 5.3 |
| 5 | 3.30 | 0.106 | 1.5 |
| 1000 | 3.33 | 0.0689 | 1.0 |

Inside the controlled state space [x_L, y*] = [1.026, 1.74] the solvency branch
holds only on [1.026, 1.168], about 17 percent of it. The 85 percent of
"exposure at the cap" is mostly the liquidity branch, where zeta falls from
3.14 to 0.36 and is never near nu_3^2 = 0.0688 (5.3 times it at y*). The
dividend barrier reflects the state at y*, so x > y* is never visited. The
manuscript's statement that the solve supports "the cap binds near both ends"
(Appendix B, K2 scope note, "Illustrations") is true of the exposure being at
the cap but does not support the surplus limit as a feature of the bank's
buffer variance. M11 and M12 are correct as theorems. What they say about the
controlled bank is only the deficit side: nu_1^2 on [x_L, xbar], and the M12
bound max(sigma_L^2, nu_1^2) = 3.14 everywhere.

F5 CONFIRMED. pi = 3.2 to 3.3 over most of the inaction region, so the solve
is the leveraged case of C3, which C3 itself calls the mathematical benchmark
rather than the main banking case (no borrowing, pi <= 1). In that case the
liquidity branch is replaced by the ceiling pi x <= x and the surplus
limit does not arise. The solve therefore illustrates K1's hypotheses in the
leveraged case only. Whether they hold with pi <= 1 is not shown.

F6 (GAP, Appendix B). The finite differences of the solve are quantised by the
grid (step 0.0025 at 601 nodes, 0.00125 at 1201). dxL_dK by finite difference
is -0.328 at 601 and -0.482 at 1201 against -0.385 and -0.430 by formula, so
the magnitudes are not converged; only the signs are supported. The text
"determinant -0.30 against -0.33" reads as a magnitude check and should say
signs.

F4 (still OPEN). "W'(x_L)>0 holds exactly when rho_L W(y_post) < R - x_L" is
not checked anywhere in Lean and the solve does not test it. Needs either a
derivation or removal.

F7 (OPEN, to check in Pass 6). Table `tab:common-values` row for Black and
Perold states the limits "256/81 and 16/225" with no liability shock, and the
text says the unhedged shock lowers the deficit limit by 0.6 percent and the
surplus limit by 3.2 percent. Recompute: (3.1401-3.1605)/3.1605 = -0.65 percent
and (0.0688-0.0711)/0.0711 = -3.3 percent.

## Pass 4, the solve with no borrowing, 28 Oct 2026

Author's decision (28 Oct): keep K2 as it is and add a solve with pi <= 1 to
test it. `solve/run_noborrow.py` (new) imposes pi <= 1 on the same model,
parameters and scheme, without editing `solve_qvi.py` (whose pinned results
stay valid), and writes `solve/results_noborrow.md`, pinned; TH-6 of
`verify_theory.py` now checks the pins of both results files (tested: a
changed script fails TH-6, the restored one passes).

Results, pi <= 1:

- At (lambda_S, K) = (1.2, 0.05) every hypothesis of M1, M2 and M4 holds on 601
  and 1201 nodes. Thresholds y* = 1.251, x_L = 1.006, y_post = 1.218, against
  1.741, 1.026, 1.553 in the leveraged case. The responses have the signs of
  M1 and M2; determinant -0.060 by formula and -0.094 by finite difference at
  1201 nodes (-0.060 and -0.078 at 601), so again signs only.
- Over the 56 values of (lambda_S, K) the trigger is interior at 27 and at the
  distress boundary at 29 (leveraged case: 42 and 14). Every hypothesis holds
  at the 27 interior points. K1 applies on about half the parameter grid
  rather than three quarters; it fails for every K at lambda_S <= 1.05 and for
  large K at higher lambda_S.
- Buffer variance on [x_L, y*]. The solvency branch binds on [x_L, 1.047] only
  (the ceiling pi <= 1 takes over at x = 1/(1-a_1)), where zeta = nu_1^2 =
  3.14. The exposure is below the cap on about [1.06, 1.17] and at the ceiling
  pi = 1 near y*. zeta at y* is 0.155, 2.25 times nu_3^2 and 24 times
  Q(1) = 0.0063. The share of the inaction region with pi at the cap is 0.50.
  The bound of M12 holds: max zeta on the path is nu_1^2.

What this says about K2, kept as written:

- K2's hypothesis, that the optimal exposure binds the cap of A2 near both
  ends, fails in the no-borrowing case at the upper end, where the binding
  constraint is the ceiling pi <= 1 and not the liquidity branch. This is
  what C3 says in words; the solve now shows it.
- Where the exposure is below the cap (about a fifth of the region here), zeta
  depends on the value function and so on lambda_S. K2's "free of lambda_S"
  holds only where a constraint binds.
- The deficit limit is attained on [x_L, 1.047], and the M12 bound holds on
  the whole path, in both cases.
- In neither case is the surplus limit approached by the controlled bank
  (F2): 5.3 times nu_3^2 at y* leveraged, 2.25 times without borrowing.

Not yet in the manuscript. Appendix B's Illustrations paragraph and K2's scope
note describe the leveraged solve only; text for the no-borrowing solve is
